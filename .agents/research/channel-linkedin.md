# Person-led LinkedIn posts about a developer tool

Question: what must a person-led LinkedIn post about a developer tool contain, in which format, with which link placement and disclosure, so that LinkedIn distributes it and the post breaks none of LinkedIn's rules?

The target artifact is the `linkedin-post` skill and its facts file (`channel-topic-map.md`, Group 2). Provenance labels: **LI** means LinkedIn's own page. **LI-API** means LinkedIn developer documentation on Microsoft Learn. **GOV** means a regulator. **3P** means vendor or third-party data. **STD** means a standards body. LinkedIn Help pages show only a relative age ("Last updated: N ago") as of the fetch date, 2026-11-27, and those ages are quoted as stated.

## Verdict

1. **A person publishes and the agent drafts.** The agent's output is a draft file with a metadata header. It never posts, schedules, comments, reposts, or loads member content on linkedin.com. The User Agreement, section 8.2 (effective 3 Nov 2025), bars scraping tools (item 2), bypassing use limits (item 3), "bots or other unauthorized automated methods" that "create, comment on, like, share, or re-share posts" (item 13) and overlays (item 15) ([User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)). That wording was read from the served HTML on 2026-11-27, not from a fetch-tool summary (command in CH-LI-01). Accounts that use such tools "risk having their accounts restricted or shut down" ([a1341387](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387), "2 years ago").
2. **The post comes from a person. Whether a company Page ever exists is an owner decision.** No company Page was found for the fleet, but the audit could not prove absence (`product-audit/distribution-posture.md:229`). No dataset measures Page against person for clicks on a developer tool, so the evidence supports neither choice. Page-only features such as collaboration invites and RSS import are not reach levers ([li-format-and-reach-evidence](channel-linkedin/li-format-and-reach-evidence.md) section 2). CH-LI-20 is a routing rule, not a finding against Pages.
3. **Lines 1 to 3 stay within 140 graphemes, and the author's connection to the tool sits in line 1 or 2.** LinkedIn publishes no "see more" cut-off. Six of eight vendor pages (March to November 2026) put the mobile cut at about 140 characters. Pollen says 210. Supergrow alone says 2 lines and about 90 to 140 characters ([li-limits-and-fold](channel-linkedin/li-limits-and-fold.md) section 3, grade C minus). The 140 figure is the consensus minimum and a SHOULD (CH-LI-03). The connection placement is a MUST because the FTC bars a disclosure behind "MORE" (CH-LI-04). Line 1 or 2 also survives the Supergrow 2-line outlier.
4. **The link goes in the body as plain text, once, after line 3, in a text, document or video post, with no preview card.** The best-controlled dataset is a vendor study of 865,980 posts that compares each creator with their own posts ([MagicPost, 29 Oct 2026](https://magicpost.in/blog/linkedin-external-links-reach), 3P). A preview card as the only visual cost 35% of impressions. A URL typed in a text, carousel or video post was not distinguishable from zero. A URL in an image post measured -5%. A link in the first comment differed by -2%, which the study calls no measurable difference. The same vendor publishes two other readings of a typed URL. Its 1.2M-post article (5 Jun 2026, pooled medians on 566,957 posts) says a URL in the body "carries no penalty" (858 against 795 median impressions) ([MagicPost](https://magicpost.in/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026), 3P). Its 687,000-post same-creator study puts a URL in text at -12% before mid-May 2026 and -21% since, a card at -39% then -19%, and a "link in the comments" line at -8% then -16% ([MagicPost, 6 Oct 2026](https://magicpost.in/studies/linkedin-algorithm-changes), 3P; [followup-q0](channel-linkedin/followup-q0.md)). That study states no pitch control, no link-post count and no interval for its link rows. **Documented gap:** MagicPost does not reconcile its three baselines (about 0%, -2%, -21%), no independent dataset was found, and LinkedIn has published no link-reach figure. Settling the size needs a controlled experiment on posts the fleet owns, which needs a human and a logged-in account. No placement escapes a cost in the 687,000-post data, and no study counts clicks. The body placement therefore rests on measurability: LinkedIn analytics do not count clicks on a link in a comment. Image plus typed URL waits on manual case 11, because LinkedIn says "You can share either a URL link or an image in a post, but not both at the same time" ([a527229](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a527229), "1 day ago").
5. **Every post declares a goal and a metric that exists. The format follows the goal by convention.** LinkedIn publishes no format ranking. The vendor benchmarks rank formats differently depending on the metric, they measure company Pages, and none of them counts clicks ([Socialinsider, 16 Mar 2026, modified 6 Nov 2026](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin), 3P). Declaring the goal and metric is a MUST (CH-LI-02). Matching the format to the table below is a SHOULD (CH-LI-22), because every cell is a convention graded C or D.
6. **Specific first-hand content is the distribution strategy.** LinkedIn says content that is "generic, repetitive, or lacks a clear point of view is less likely to be widely distributed". It also says AI-assisted content "is welcome when it reflects a real person's perspective, experience, or expertise" ([a1481496](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1481496), "1 month ago"). An agent cannot supply first-hand material. A human supplies it with its source, or the draft stops.
7. **No pods or coordinated engagement (policy). No engagement bait (reach).** LinkedIn's newsroom says pods and automated comments "aren't allowed" ([news.linkedin.com, 13 Mar 2026](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/authentic-content-and-conversations)). The policies bar agreeing "ahead of time to like or re-share" ([Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies), no date shown). No LinkedIn page bans "comment KEYWORD" by name, and the only reach figure for it comes from a paywalled report this program did not read. The bait rule (CH-LI-13) is therefore a SHOULD. The DM automation the pattern invites is barred by UA 8.2 item 13.
8. **Every LinkedIn number comes from a dated facts file.** Facts are refreshed every 180 days for hard limits and every 90 days for policy and for third-party figures. The fold budget is a third-party figure.

## The ruleset

All rules apply to any LinkedIn post or document draft that an agent writes for a named person, except CH-LI-18, which applies to the skill and its facts file. "The check script" means `check.py` in the appendix of [li-limits-and-fold](channel-linkedin/li-limits-and-fold.md), run as `python3 -I check.py fixtures.json`. "The pattern script" means `run.py` in the appendix of [li-policy-and-ai-content](channel-linkedin/li-policy-and-ai-content.md), with the emoji pattern added as CH-LI-13 states. "The brief" is the input file the human gives the agent, holding `source:` lines.

## AI-agent failure modes

Ranked by how often each one shows up in agent drafts, grouped by mechanism.

1. **Genre imitation of "LinkedIn style"** (most frequent). The agent opens with "Excited to announce", writes one sentence per line with blank lines between, decorates with emoji, uses Unicode bold, puts the disclosure in a `#ad` or `#colleague` hashtag at the end, and closes with "Agree? Drop a 🔥". Checks: CH-LI-03, -04, -06, -13.
2. **Training-data folklore stated as fact.** Examples: "put the link in the first comment", "the first hour decides reach", "210 characters desktop, 140 mobile" quoted as LinkedIn's rule, the pre-2023 1,300-character limit, "AI posts are suppressed", "561% more reach than a Page". A newer variant picks one vendor figure out of a conflicting set, such as "links cost 21%", and states it as the effect. Checks: CH-LI-18 provenance grep, CH-LI-09 and CH-LI-23.
3. **Invented specifics to satisfy a specificity rule.** CH-LI-07 creates this pressure. The agent invents a benchmark, a customer quote, or a "40% faster". Checks: the CH-LI-07 `source:` line, CH-LI-08 traceability and `reviewed_by`. An agent with no source must ask.
4. **Attachment omissions.** Empty alt text on the belief that LinkedIn adds it. A PDF carousel treated as accessible because it is a PDF. A bare URL whose card has no `og:image` (the ocx.sh case). Checks: CH-LI-10, -11, -12.
5. **Over-reach in action.** The agent proposes a scheduler or an auto-comment tool, a launch-day "everyone like and comment" message to the team, a repost rota, posting through the API, or fetching the live post to "check how it looks". Checks: CH-LI-01, CH-LI-14.
6. **Reusing fleet copy unchanged.** The agent pastes commit-subject release notes as bullets, or lifts "fast, cross-platform" from the GitHub description. Checks: CH-LI-15, CH-LI-07.
7. **Counting errors.** Measuring length with `len()` or JavaScript `.length`. The 3,000-character boundary and the fold budget then break on emoji and combining marks (F03: 98 UTF-16 units against 74 graphemes). Checks: CH-LI-03, CH-LI-06.
8. **Metric theatre.** The agent adds UTM tags where nothing records them, or reports engagement rate as success for a repository-traffic goal. Check: CH-LI-02.

## What an agent must ask a human

- Who posts, from which profile, and when. Posting is a human act.
- The author's exact tie to the tool, and the disclosure wording for the markets the post reaches. The marketing sub-program owns the legal standard.
- The first-hand element: the real number, its command and output, the failure, or the trade-off. Also the source of every quote and the consent of every person mentioned.
- Whether value was exchanged, so the Brand partnership toggle is needed.
- Whether the human rewrote the draft (`human_rewrite`), and the owner's AI-disclosure policy if they want the default line off.
- Approval of any poll wording before the poll is created.
- Whether colleagues may be asked to share. The agent never produces a schedule or a list.
- Whether to create a company Page. The evidence does not decide it.
- Whether to use any third-party scheduler, preview tool or extension. The default is no. LinkedIn's own composer is the safe scheduler.
- Whether to add an owned redirect or analytics so clicks can be counted. The fleet's no-analytics stance is deliberate.
- Whether the author's profile is verified and its headline names the post's topic. New accounts are shown narrowly ([a16425028](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a16425028), "1 month ago").
