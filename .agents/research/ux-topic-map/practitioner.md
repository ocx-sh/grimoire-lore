# UX practitioner corpus: landscape scout

Scope: argued positions in practitioner writing (NN/g, Baymard, Smashing, A List Apart, Adam Silver, Luke Wroblewski, Linear, Stripe, Vercel, Shape Up, Josh Comeau, WebAIM, W3C). Pages were opened with WebFetch on 2026-11-27. No local measurements were taken, so there are no measurement commands in this file. Not reached (page empty, 404 or 403): Built for Mars, Brad Frost, UX Collective, Erika Hall primary text, Growth.Design primary text, the EUR-Lex directive text. Claims drawn only from search snippets are marked "snippet" and must be re-fetched before a rule cites them.

## Findings that shape the candidate list

1. The most checkable guidance in the corpus is the Vercel web interface guidelines: about 70 rules a linter or reviewer can test (focus-visible, 24px/44px targets, 16px mobile input font, `prefers-reduced-motion`, no blocked paste, deep-linkable filters). Source: https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines (page shows no date; 60 commits). Part is brand preference ("Vercel-Specific" section) and must not be adopted as universal.
2. Accessibility is a measured, legal and cheap-to-check surface: 95.9% of top 1M home pages had detectable WCAG 2 failures in Feb 2026, and the top failures (contrast, alt text, labels, empty links/buttons) are all machine-detectable. https://webaim.org/projects/million/
3. Positions that depend on context, not truth, are the main risk for an agent that follows rules literally: inline validation, hidden navigation, five-user tests, APCA vs WCAG 2 contrast. See "Contested".
4. Practitioner evidence decays unevenly. NN/g articles carry "last reviewed" dates years after publication (F-pattern published 2017, reviewed 2026-08-19), so a review date is not a re-test date.
5. The corpus is e-commerce and consumer-app heavy (Baymard). Developer-tool surfaces (CLI, docs, install flows) are covered by clig.dev and NN/g heuristics, not by Baymard-style benchmarks. Transfer is an assumption to flag in any rule.
