# UX canonical guides: landscape scout

## Method and limits

The repair pass re-fetched every page that a candidate or dated fact rests on. The 45 pages are listed at the end under "Sources fetched" and the count comes from this command, run on that list saved as a file: `wc -l < fetched.txt` printed `45`. All were opened with WebFetch on 2026-11-27 and returned content. No other measurement was taken.

What the repair changed:

- Removed off-axis rows: the seven WCAG 2.2 success-criterion rows, the European Accessibility Act row and the clig.dev CLI row. They belong to the accessibility-standards and CLI corpora, not to the interface and interaction canon. Where a canon source touches the same ground (touch target size, color-only cues) the candidate now rests on the canon source, and the WCAG number is left to the accessibility scout.
- Removed the false claim that touch and motion criteria are new in WCAG 2.2, and the garbled motion row. No WCAG claim remains in this file.
- Added the canon that was missing: Tog's principle list in full, Shneiderman's rules verbatim, Johnson's chapter list, the Laws of UX list, Refactoring UI chapters, and the NN/g articles on clickability, icons, tooltips, modals, error forms, mental models, visual hierarchy, recognition and recall, and scanning.

Unreadable in this pass (HTTP 403, 404 or SSL error on the first-party or publisher page; the session web-search budget was exhausted, so no alternative route was tried):

- Cooper, About Face: the Wiley page returned 403, the Wikipedia article 404, the Wikipedia Alan Cooper page is a disambiguation page. No primary text of any edition. Cooper-derived candidates are not made; the concepts (personas, posture, excise) are named only as unverified.
- Tidwell, Designing Interfaces: the O'Reilly pages returned 403, the first-edition pattern list failed with an SSL error, the Wikipedia article 404. The pattern catalogue is not surveyed. Needs a deep-dive that can obtain the table of contents.
- Wroblewski, Web Form Design: the Luke W page loaded but has no chapter list or recommendations; the Rosenfeld Media page returned 403. Wroblewski's content is represented only by NN/g form articles, not by the book. Mobile First: only the publisher blurb (A Book Apart) loaded, no chapters.
- Norman, The Design of Everyday Things: only the NN/g book page and the Wikipedia article loaded. The NN/g page does not use the words affordance, signifier or conceptual model. Norman's principles are therefore not primary here.
- Krug, Don't Make Me Think: the publisher page and Wikipedia loaded; neither lists chapters or the trunk test. Not represented beyond edition facts.
- Gestalt: no primary Gestalt text. Represented by the NN/g proximity article and the Laws of UX common-region page.
- Several NN/g URLs guessed from memory returned 404 (principles-of-design-everyday-things, natural-mapping, mobile-touch-target-size, inline-validation) and were not used.

## What the canon says an agent would violate

Most of the canon (about four in five of the 80 Tog statements and all of Nielsen's heuristics as phrased) is judgment no tool can check. The usable residue is what becomes a number, a markup rule, or a state that exists or does not.

| Source | Principles an agent writing interface code would violate | Checkable in code or a rendered page |
|---|---|---|
| Nielsen's 10 heuristics (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) | 1 status (no pending or success state), 3 exit (no cancel or undo), 5 prevention (destructive action, no confirm or undo), 6 recognition (command or gesture with no visible cue), 9 errors (raw error text), 10 help (none in context) | 1, 3, 5, 9 by the existence of a state or handler; 6, 10 partly; 2, 4, 7, 8 by reading only |
