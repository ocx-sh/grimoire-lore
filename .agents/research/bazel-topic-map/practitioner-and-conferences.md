## Summary

- Bazel 9.0 (shipped January 2026) **completely removed WORKSPACE support code** — not just disabled by default (as in Bazel 8) — so any guidance that still shows `WORKSPACE`/`workspace()` as a live option is historical-only past this point.
- The Bzlmod migration is a multi-part, still-being-written-about problem as late as May 2025 (EngFlow's five-part series): the "easy parts" (dependency lifting) are genuinely easy, but module extensions, toolchain registration, and canonical-repo-name handling are the hard, still-underdocumented parts.
- The single strongest rule-authoring lesson from the bzlmod corpus: **never depend on a canonical repository name** (the `@@name~version` string) — treat it as an implementation detail; use `repository_ctx.original_name` (Bazel ≥8.1.0) and `Label()` wrapping instead. Rule/macro code that hardcodes canonical names breaks silently across Bazel versions.
- Practitioners disagree sharply on CI target-selection strategy: Aspect argues predictive tools (`bazel-diff`, target-determinator) are either "very incorrect" or "very slow" and recommends an empirical "shared green" whole-repo pipeline instead; Tinder's own BazelCon 2025 talk reports `bazel-diff` giving them a 93% CI time reduction. Both camps are active in 2025 — this is unresolved, not settled.
- Canva's CI rebuild (2022-2024) is the fullest publicly quantified before/after case study in this corpus: 80 minutes → under 30 minutes, ~3,000 CI jobs collapsed to hundreds of pipeline lines, ~$1.8M/year claimed savings, achieved by combining Build without the Bytes (`--remote_download_minimal`) with a custom `bazel-diff`-based input-hashing layer uploaded to S3.
- Build without the Bytes (BwoB) is not free of failure modes: BuildBuddy documents a specific bug class where a long-lived Bazel server (their MicroVM runners) outlives the remote cache's artifact TTL (default 3h), and Bazel silently falls back to downloading the *entire* artifact — one build's downloads jumped from 1.8MB to 640MB. The practitioner fix is `--experimental_remote_cache_ttl=10000d`, a workaround, not a real solution.
- Farid Zakaria (Confluent, BazelCon Simplicity Steering Group) argues Bazel carries three "original sins" baked in from its Google-internal origin: (1) mounting `/` read-only into the sandbox by default, so external teams silently depend on host libraries Google never had to worry about; (2) Unix-only design (no native Windows symlinks, forcing runfiles manifests); (3) reinventing third-party dependency management (Bzlmod) that Google's curated monorepo never needed. His framing: Bazel's tools are not "silver bullets" — their design assumptions came from Google's specific environment.
- Uber's Go monorepo case study credits Bazel + Gazelle (auto-generated BUILD files) and a hermetic Go/C toolchain for scaling from <100 to ~10,000 monthly commits and <10 to ~900 active engineers on ~70,000 Go files, but names IDE support and target-affected-detection as still-unsolved, home-grown-tooling problems even after adoption.
- IDE/LSP support for Bazel is an acknowledged, ongoing gap as of BazelCon 2025: JetBrains reports needing to "clone Bazel and build each tool manually" for the Execution Log Parser and Starlark REPL, and points to `starpls` (a Rust-based Starlark language server) as the emerging community answer rather than an existing solved feature.
- Action deduplication and remote-execution routing are treated as advanced, high-leverage but non-default RBE topics in 2024-2025 talks: Canva reports "500k action dedups/day," and BuildBuddy's 2026 "Action Merging" and "Snapshot, Chunk, Clone" posts describe scheduler-side techniques most teams never touch because they require operating their own RE backend.
- Symbolic macros (Bazel 8) got a mixed reception even inside Google: BazelCon 2024 recap notes internal "pushback" over fear that lazy-evaluated macros will encourage larger, harder-to-navigate BUILD packages — a genuine trade-off, not a uniformly celebrated feature.
- Path mapping (cross-configuration cache reuse, e.g. one Java build serving both arm64 and x86 target platforms) and Skymeld (interleaved analysis/execution) were both called out repeatedly across 2023-2025 conference recaps as the performance levers with the best evidence behind them, more so than remote execution alone for smaller shops.
- The predictive-vs-empirical CI debate has a determinism precondition neither side disputes: Canva's own BazelCon 2025 talk is literally titled "Determinism in a Non-Hermetic World," and their earlier CI-rebuild post names concrete non-hermetic culprits (shared `localstack` containers for backend integration tests, a11y test suites) that had to be carved out before any target-selection strategy could be trusted.
- A community governance shift is underway, not just a technical one: BazelCon moved to Linux Foundation stewardship in 2024, and by BazelCon 2025 a "BUILD Foundation" had three founding members (Spotify, Uber, Canva) explicitly seeking to reduce single-vendor (Google) control — this is a trust/roadmap-stability signal relevant to any "why adopt Bazel" argument.
- Windows-on-Bazel coverage is thin in this corpus but consistent: third-party rulesets (not Bazel core) are named as the real Unix-isms problem, and CLion's April 2025 plugin release calling out "Windows support" as a headline new feature is itself evidence the gap was still open in 2025.
- rules_js/rules_ts (Aspect) is presented as a from-scratch rewrite specifically to fix analysis-phase performance the older `@bazel/typescript`/rules_nodejs could not: pnpm's lockfile is mirrored into Starlark so Bazel's own repository-rule fetcher (not npm/pnpm at build time) does dependency fetching, and rules_js 2.0 further shrank the third-party depset and sped up `npm_translate_lock`.
- Python hermeticity in this corpus is framed as an opt-in you must explicitly request: `pip_parse` and `py_binary`/`py_test` only get a pinned interpreter if you call `python_register_toolchains()` (a rules_python feature since 0.7.0, 2022) — silently omitting it is the default footgun. `uv` integration (rules_python's experimental `uv_toolchain`, and the standalone `rules_uv`) is a 2024-2025 addition still marked experimental.
- rules_rust's crate_universe exposes the vendor-vs-fetch choice as two first-class rules rather than a single recommended path: `crates_repository` (fetch at analysis time, `CARGO_BAZEL_REPIN` to regenerate the lock) versus `crates_vendor` (materialize crates into the workspace) — the corpus does not show a converged practitioner recommendation between them.
- Bazel's own "original sin" critique and the bzlmod migration-guide pain both point to the same underlying theme this program should treat as a P0 pattern: **anything that leaks a non-portable identity (a canonical repo name, a host filesystem path, a Unix assumption) into rule/macro logic breaks later, quietly, and is expensive to find** — determinism-adjacent, not just a Bzlmod-specific lesson.

### 5. Bzlmod migration guide (official) — [bazel.build/external/migration](https://bazel.build/external/migration)
Official doc, primary source. Concrete pre-migration step: `bazel clean --expunge && bazel sync --experimental_repository_resolved_file=resolved.bzl` to inventory every existing external dependency before touching MODULE.bazel. Names the hybrid path (`WORKSPACE.bzlmod` alongside `WORKSPACE`, gated by `common --enable_bzlmod` in `.bazelrc`), the `bind()`-is-deprecated note, and `local_path_override()` as the bzlmod replacement for `local_repository`. States the same two deadlines the community sources independently confirm: WORKSPACE disabled by default in Bazel 8 (late 2024), removed for Bazel 9 (late 2025 target, shipped January 2026).

### 6-9. EngFlow "Migrating to Bazel Modules (a.k.a. Bzlmod)" series — [The Easy Parts](https://blog.engflow.com/2024/06/27/migrating-to-bazel-modules-aka-bzlmod---the-easy-parts/) (Jun 2024), [Module Extensions](https://blog.engflow.com/2025/01/16/migrating-to-bazel-modules-aka-bzlmod---module-extensions/) (Jan 2025), [Repo Names, Again…](https://blog.engflow.com/2025/04/17/migrating-to-bazel-modules-aka-bzlmod---repo-names-againhellip/) (Apr 2025), [Toolchainization](https://blog.engflow.com/2025/05/14/migrating-to-bazel-modules-aka-bzlmod---toolchainization/) (May 2025)
A four-part (of a longer) series that is the single most detailed practitioner treatment of bzlmod migration mechanics found. Key claims:
- **Easy parts**: gradual/incremental migration works without an intermediate `WORKSPACE.bzlmod` for simple apps; a `bazel_dep()` from BCR can replace "thirty-eight lines of WORKSPACE configuration" with three; version resolution via MVS (minimal version selection) is strictly better than WORKSPACE's order-dependent first-wins behavior.
- **Hard parts named**: `load()` is banned inside `MODULE.bazel`; module extensions cannot both instantiate a repo and load a file from it in the same extension (`Circular definition of repositories` error) — forcing rule sets like `rules_scala` to split `scala_config()` into a separate extension from `scala_repositories()`; `native.register_toolchains()` / `native.bind()` are unavailable inside extensions.
- **Toolchainization pattern**: to avoid forcing end users to `use_repo()` toolchain internals they never reference directly, rule-set maintainers should generate one dedicated toolchain repo inside their own extension and call `register_toolchains()` themselves — shifting bzlmod's verbosity from consumer to maintainer. Names `module_ctx.extension_metadata(root_module_direct_deps = "all")`, `dev_dependency = True`, and (for protoc specifically) `--incompatible_enable_proto_toolchain_resolution`.
- **Repo-names**: the Prime Directive is "only refer to a repository by its apparent name, not its canonical name" — canonical names (`@@name~version`) are an implementation detail Bazel is free to reshape between versions (and did, in 7.1.0, dropping version encoding from the canonical form). Recommends `repository_ctx.original_name` (Bazel ≥8.1.0), `Label()` wrapping instead of string concatenation, and `native.package_relative_label()` inside macros.

### 10. BuildBuddy — "Unusual Builds with Bytes" (Son Luong Ngoc, Mar 2025) — [buildbuddy.io/blog/unusual-builds-w-bytes](https://www.buildbuddy.io/blog/unusual-builds-w-bytes/)
Documents a specific BwoB failure mode for long-lived Bazel servers (their own MicroVM-based remote runners): with `--remote_download_minimal`, Bazel trusts remote-cache metadata for a TTL window (default 3 hours; `--experimental_remote_cache_ttl`); once the TTL expires mid-session, instead of re-validating remotely, Bazel silently downloads the *entire* artifact to disk, observed jumping a build's download size from 1.8MB to 640MB. Practitioner workaround: set the TTL absurdly high (`10000d`, ~27 years). Also names `--experimental_remote_cache_eviction_retries=5` as the retry default since Bazel 8.0.0 and `--remote_download_regex` for selective downloads.

### 11. Aspect — "Monorepo Shared Green" — [aspect.build/blog/monorepo-shared-green](https://aspect.build/blog/monorepo-shared-green)
Direct rebuttal of predictive CI target-selection: calls Tinder's `bazel-diff` "very incorrect" (will silently miss affected targets — cites a linked GitHub issue) and target-determinator "very slow," and even calls Google's internal Skyframe-based approach "occasionally incorrect." Argues the empirical alternative — one shared, whole-monorepo build-and-test pipeline gating every deploy — needs no target-prediction code at all and "is easy for engineers to reason about." This is the strongest single-source statement of the predictive-vs-empirical CI split (see Contested).

### 12. Aspect — "Python toolchains in rules_python" — [aspect.build/blog/python-toolchains](https://aspect.build/blog/python-toolchains)
Explains that Bazel's repeatability guarantee for Python only holds if you opt into `python_register_toolchains()` (available since rules_python 0.7.0, March 2022) — without it, `py_binary`/`py_test` fall back to whatever interpreter is on the host PATH, silently non-hermetic. Notes the awkward ordering problem: `pip_parse()` runs as a repository rule, which executes *before* toolchain resolution, so the resolved interpreter must be threaded in explicitly via `@python_version_resolved_interpreter//:defs.bzl` and the `python_interpreter_target` argument — an easy step to skip.

### 13. Uber Engineering — "Building Uber's Go Monorepo with Bazel" — [uber.com/us/en/blog/go-monorepo-bazel](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/go-monorepo-bazel/)
Company case study, primary source. Scale numbers: growth from <100 to ~10,000 monthly commits and from <10 to ~900 active engineers, across 70,000+ Go files, on Make/`go build` infrastructure that could not do incremental or distributed builds. Bazel + Gazelle (auto-generated BUILD files, with Uber-contributed features: separated generated-external-module macro files, automatic obsolete-dependency pruning) plus a hermetic Go/C toolchain were the load-bearing decisions. Explicitly names ongoing pain even post-adoption: IDE support for Bazel-generated Go packages remained inadequate, and target-affected detection required home-grown tooling outside Bazel's own ecosystem — i.e., adopting Bazel did not itself solve the CI target-selection problem (converges with the Aspect/Tinder debate above).

### 14. Canva Engineering — "Faster continuous integration builds at Canva" — [canva.dev/blog/engineering/faster-ci-builds-at-canva](https://www.canva.dev/blog/engineering/faster-ci-builds-at-canva/)
Company case study with the fullest quantified before/after in this corpus. Before (April 2022): ~80-minute builds (some 3+ hours), ~3,000 CI jobs (1 EC2 instance/job), petabytes/week of dependency downloads, a 900K+-node build graph, and non-hermetic ad hoc scripts (missing input declarations) blocking caching entirely. Fixes: BwoB via `--remote_download_minimal` (2-3x fewer downloads), then a custom `bazel-diff`-based pipeline hashing every target's inputs and staging results in S3 to select only affected jobs. Named non-hermetic blockers that had to be fixed first: shared `localstack` containers for backend integration tests, and accessibility test suites alone consuming 680K CI-minutes/month. Result (by September 2023): builds under 30 minutes, ~50% fewer compute-minutes, 90 frontend-integration jobs eliminated per commit, ~$1.8M/year claimed savings.

### 15-17. Independent BazelCon trip reports (Julio Merino, Snowflake — Blog System/5) — [2023](https://blogsystem5.substack.com/p/bazelcon-2023-et-al-trip-report), [2024](https://blogsystem5.substack.com/p/bazelcon-2024-recap), [2025](https://blogsystem5.substack.com/p/bazelcon-2025-recap)
Independent (non-vendor) year-over-year synthesis by a working Bazel engineer (also a 2025 BazelCon speaker himself, "The Curious Case of Slow Java Tests After Moving to Bazel," Snowflake). Cross-year numbers and claims: 2023 — Salesforce's migration covered "4000 engineers" and "70% have trouble writing BUILD files" (motivating Gazelle-style automation); EngFlow and Bloomberg both independently found gRPC compression *worse* for remote execution performance; regional RE cluster placement gave EngFlow a measured 10-20% build-time improvement. 2024 — a cited empirical study found 11% of Bazel adopters abandon it around the two-year mark, mostly for language-native tooling (Go) or "inferior" but simpler systems (CMake); Airbnb saw builds run 2x slower without remote persistent workers; Uber found 71% of changes touch only source files (a target-selection heuristic); peak memory dropped 95% after a build, 25-30% overall, per the "Reducing Bazel's Memory Consumption" talk; symbolic macros drew "pushback inside Google" over encouraging oversized packages. 2025 — Skycache claimed cold builds 46s → 13s via Skyframe serialization; path stripping claimed "up to 84%" build-time reduction; a NestedSets change saved "up to 20%" memory; the BUILD Foundation had three founding members (Spotify, Uber, Canva); Google's `#general` Bazel Slack grew 18% to 8,500 members; ~10,800 GitHub repos contain a `MODULE.bazel`; Tinder's `bazel-diff` reported "93% time savings" — directly contradicting Aspect's "very incorrect" framing (see Contested).

### 18. Farid Zakaria — "Bazel's Original Sins" (Jun 2025) — [fzakaria.com/2025/06/22/bazel-s-original-sins](https://fzakaria.com/2025/06/22/bazel-s-original-sins)
Independent practitioner (Confluent; also runs BazelCon's "Simplicity Steering Group," per the 2024 schedule) critique. Names three foundational design choices that cause pain specifically *outside* Google: (1) sandboxes mount `/` read-only by default, so builds silently depend on host system libraries — the author traces a real bug to a GNU-vs-BSD `diff` mismatch; (2) Bazel was designed Unix-first with no native Windows symlink support, forcing the runfiles-manifest workaround still in use; (3) Bzlmod reinvents semantic-version dependency resolution that Google's own centrally-curated `//third_party` never had to solve, so it re-imports the version-conflict problems Bazel's reputation was partly built on avoiding. Thesis: Bazel's tooling reflects Google's specific internal environment, not universal correctness — treat "just adopt Bazel" claims skeptically.

### 19. JetBrains/CLion — "I Went to BazelCon 2025" — [blog.jetbrains.com/clion/2025/11/bazelcon-2025](https://blog.jetbrains.com/clion/2025/11/bazelcon-2025/)
Tool-vendor conference recap focused on the IDE/tooling angle largely absent elsewhere in this corpus. States plainly that today's workflow for using the Execution Log Parser or Starlark REPL requires cloning Bazel and building each tool manually — i.e., no packaged distribution exists. Names `starpls` (a Rust-based Starlark LSP) as the community's emerging, not-yet-standard answer, and `gazelle_cc` as an active BUILD-file generator for C++. Names an MCP server exposing BCR dependency data to AI assistants as a 2025 addition — one of the only AI-tooling-adjacent items in the whole corpus.

## Recent shifts seen in this corpus

- **WORKSPACE: disabled-by-default (Bazel 8, late 2024) → code fully removed (Bazel 9.0, January 2026).** Any pre-2024 guidance showing WORKSPACE as a live path is now wrong, not just discouraged. [Bazel 9 LTS](https://blog.bazel.build/2026/01/20/bazel-9.html)
- **CI target selection moved from "which tool is best" to an active two-camp split with fresh 2026 re-engagement.** Aspect published new posts on the topic as late as 2026 ("Which tests are affected? Ask the cache, run nothing.") while Tinder's BazelCon 2025 talk defended `bazel-diff` with a specific 93% number — this is not a settled debate as of the research date. [Aspect blog index](https://aspect.build/blog/)
- **Both major RE-tooling vendors pivoted their 2025-2026 blog output toward AI-agent-driven remote execution** (BuildBuddy: "Remote Bazel," "Configuring Remote Bazel with agents"; Aspect: "RBE is fast enough for AI agents. But, is it safe enough?") — a genuinely new framing that didn't exist in the 2023-2024 corpus. [BuildBuddy blog index](https://www.buildbuddy.io/blog/)
- **Governance decentralization**: BazelCon moved from a Google-run event to Linux Foundation stewardship in 2024, and a vendor-and-adopter-founded "BUILD Foundation" (Spotify, Uber, Canva) appeared by BazelCon 2025 — invalidates any older framing of Bazel as a purely Google-controlled project. [BazelCon 2025 schedule](https://bazelcon2025.sched.com/)
- **rules_js/rules_ts underwent two major version jumps in this window** (2.0, then 3.0 in Feb 2026) each explicitly framed as fixing prior-version analysis-phase performance problems — guidance pinned to rules_js 1.x internals is stale. [Aspect blog index](https://aspect.build/blog/)
- **`uv` support for Python-under-Bazel is new territory (2024-2025), still experimental**, not a mature, converged pattern — earlier guidance recommending only `pip_parse`/`rules_python` toolchains without mentioning `uv` is incomplete for a 2026-era Python-under-Bazel guide, but recommending `uv_toolchain` or `rules_uv` as stable would overclaim.
- **IDE/LSP support is actively shifting**, not stagnant: the older Google IntelliJ plugin is sunsetting ("supported until mid-2025" per the BazelCon 2024 recap) in favor of a BSP-based JetBrains plugin, while `starpls` gains traction as a separate LSP effort — a 2023-era "Bazel has no good IDE support" claim needs updating to "IDE support is fragmented across three efforts, none dominant, as of late 2025."

## Contested

- **Predictive vs. empirical CI target selection.** Aspect: `bazel-diff` is "very incorrect," target-determinator is "very slow," recommend one shared whole-repo green pipeline instead. Tinder (BazelCon 2025, per the independent recap): `bazel-diff`-based "target-aware workflows" delivered a 93% CI time reduction for them. Canva took a third path: built their own bazel-diff-based input-hashing system atop BwoB rather than adopting either vendor's off-the-shelf recommendation. **Trend**: no convergence visible through 2026 — Aspect kept publishing on the topic into 2026, suggesting the debate is still live, not resolved toward either pole. A rule in this program should present both, gated on repo scale and CI cost tolerance, not pick one side.
- **Remote-cache gRPC compression: on or off?** Both EngFlow and Bloomberg (independently, per the 2023 trip report) measured compression *hurting* remote-execution performance, contradicting the intuitive "compress = faster over the network" assumption. No corpus source pushes back on this finding — it reads as settled in the "leave compression off" direction, but the underlying evidence (a secondhand conference-recap claim, not a benchmark this scout read directly) is thinner than the confidence with which it's stated.
- **Symbolic macros: unambiguous improvement or an accepted trade-off?** Community reception at BazelCon 2024 was positive, but "some pushback inside of Google" is explicitly named over lazy evaluation encouraging larger, harder-to-navigate BUILD packages. **Trend**: adopted regardless (it shipped in Bazel 8), so the practical question shifts from "should we use it" to "how do we avoid the oversized-package failure mode it enables" — no corpus source yet offers that mitigation pattern.
- **Is Bazel's design universally sound, or specifically shaped by Google's internal environment (and therefore a worse fit for smaller, polyglot fleets)?** Most vendor and case-study sources (Aspect, EngFlow, BuildBuddy, Uber, Canva) write from an implicit "Bazel is the right tool, here's how to use it well" stance. Zakaria's "Original Sins" post is the one clear counter-voice in this corpus, arguing the tool's core assumptions (host-parity sandboxing, Unix-only symlinks, reinvented dependency resolution) came from an environment (Google's monorepo) the adopting fleet does not share. **Trend**: this is a minority position by volume in the corpus (1 post vs. many adoption case studies) but it is the single most load-bearing piece of evidence for testing the requester's hypothesis, and it comes from a person embedded in Bazel's own community governance (the Simplicity Steering Group), not an outside critic — it should not be discounted as noise.
