# Claims and evidence for product copy

Question: Which claims may product copy make, and what evidence record must exist on disk before each class of claim ships?

Scope of "copy surface" in every rule below: README files, landing and pitch pages, docs-site home pages, page titles and meta descriptions, package-manifest descriptions (`Cargo.toml`, `package.json`, `pyproject.toml`, extension manifests), the GitHub repository description, release notes, changelogs, pricing and service pages, trust and security pages, and email. Docs body pages are outside the claim gate. They keep DOC-PLAIN-12 (marketing words) and DOC-TYPE-15 (adoption-claim links) from [docs-quality](../../rules/docs-quality.md). This topic references those two rules and does not restate them.

The rules run unchanged on every surface. Whether US, EU or UK law reaches a surface depends on who the page is aimed at, and an agent never decides that. The rules are the strictest common form of the FTC, EU and UK texts read for this topic; no UK or EU rule read is looser than a US one here (followup-q1, followup-q2).

## Verdict

1. **The basis comes before the claim, and a machine enforces it.** The FTC requires a "reasonable basis" before a claim runs, and later evidence does not cure a missing one ([FTC substantiation statement, 1984-11-23](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation)). No human reviews an agent's copy, so the order is enforced by a CI gate that blocks publication. Only a human sign-off can satisfy it for some claim classes.
2. **There is one evidence store per repository: `claims.toml` at the repository root.** It holds every class of claim. Copy binds to a record by id, through a `claim:<id>` marker, by rendering the value from the record, or, on JSON and remote surfaces that cannot hold a marker, through the record's `surface` list. A record past its `refresh_by` date counts as no record.
3. **The gate keys on a narrow, measured pattern set, not on how a reader would take an adjective.** An agent cannot judge puffery, so the rule is mechanical: numbers with units, counts, prices, measurable comparatives and superlatives, compliance words, social-proof phrases, maturity grades and AI-capability words need a record or a rewrite. Universal quantifiers ("every", "all", "never") are a reading check on headline lines only. Measured on the fleet's headline corpus, the broad quantifier arm matched 170 of 2,610 lines, almost none of them claims. The narrow set matched 4 lines, and all 4 were real claim words. On the visible text of 26 exemplar home pages the ten wave-1 arms matched 326 lines, and 292 of them are real claims (89.6 %, one reader; [followup-q0](marketing-claims-evidence/followup-q0.md)). They miss about as many real claims as they find: a second sweep found 241 more real claim lines, so recall there is at most 54.8 %. Most misses are numbers the unit and count arms cannot see (`100k+`, `1.76s`, `600 KB`, "Up to 10 sites"). Five arms added in wave 2 (lines 11 to 15) catch 107 of the 241 at 90.7 % precision. The recall that results, 74.9 %, is in-sample, because the arms were written after reading the misses. What no pattern catches at useful precision (testimonial text, vague outcome words, absolutes) stays with MKT-CLAIM-08, 09, 20 and the headline reading check (re-measured 2026-11-27, commands under "Applied").
4. **Five classes are blocked outright, with no allow-list bypass:** price or limit, count, attestation or certificate, AI capability figure, and endorsement. An agent never writes one of these from memory or by inference.
5. **When no record can be built, the fix is a mechanism rewrite, never a softer adjective.** "Switch versions instantly" becomes a sentence that names what the software does and how a reader can check it. An outcome word may stay as a headline only when the same or the next sentence names the artefact and links its proof.
6. **Licence wording follows SPDX data, never memory.** "Open source" is allowed only when every option of the SPDX expression is `isOsiApproved: true` in the current `licenses.json`. The LICENSE file, the manifest, the README and the artifact frontmatter carry the same id. A licence named anywhere requires a LICENSE file in the repository.
7. **Security and compliance copy names the report or the mechanism.** "SOC 2 certified" is never written: the AICPA calls SOC outputs "assurance reports" ([AICPA](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/landing/system-and-organization-controls-soc-suite-of-services)). Compliance with a law is a legal judgement, not a copy edit.
8. **"AI-powered" is a fact about the shipped binary.** The label needs a call site that reaches a model at runtime. Using an AI assistant to build the product does not qualify ([FTC, "Keep your AI claims in check", 2023-02-27, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2023/02/keep-your-ai-claims-check)). This matters most for Grimoire, which installs AI configuration and calls no model.
9. **An agent never creates an endorsement.** Every quote, logo, badge, rating and adopter name resolves to a record that holds the person or organisation, the exact words, the capture date, the permission and the connection status. For an individual's testimonial, 16 CFR 465.2(a) names writing or creating a fake one an unfair or deceptive act ([eCFR Part 465](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465), in force 2024-10-21). An organisation's quote, logo or adopter name is outside the Part 465 testimonial sections and falls under Section 5 and the Endorsement Guides instead ([followup-q3](marketing-claims-evidence/followup-q3.md)). The EU (UCPD Annex I points 23b and 23c) and the UK (DMCC Act 2024 Schedule 20 paragraph 13, CAP Code 3.44 to 3.52) list the same fabrication as unfair in all circumstances ([followup-q1](marketing-claims-evidence/followup-q1.md), [followup-q2](marketing-claims-evidence/followup-q2.md)).
10. **The fleet's gap today is missing records, not false claims.** It states no price, no percentage, no testimonial and no compliance status. Its violations are one unmeasured speed word, one unrecorded response-time promise, four repositories with no LICENSE file (this catalog among them), no `security.txt`, and a drift table that names five third-party routes with no comparison record. One of that table's cells ("nvm: no Windows") is flatter than nvm's own README.
11. **A table that names a competing product is comparative advertising, and every cell must be a sourced, dated fact.** EU and UK law permit naming only when the comparison is objective, verifiable, about products that meet the same need, and free of denigration and of any suggested connection ([Directive 2006/114/EC Art. 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/114/article/4), [SI 2008/1276 reg. 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/1276/regulation/4), both fetched 2026-11-27). The FTC encourages truthful naming ([16 CFR 14.15](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=14&section=14.15), 1979-08-13). An agent can check truth and date on disk and can check wording. It cannot judge "same need" or "competitor", so it never adds or removes a compared party on its own (MKT-CLAIM-16, 30; [followup-q4](marketing-claims-evidence/followup-q4.md)).
12. **The EU and UK texts add seven page duties that the US text alone did not.** A page that says its reviews come from real users states whether and how that is checked (Annex I 23b, UCPD Art. 7(6)). The owner holds a pointer to each endorser's contact details (CAP 3.47). A badge record names who authorised it (CAP 3.52). A testimonial is never the evidence for an objective claim (ASA substantiation advice, 2026-09-21). A caveat sits beside the claim it limits (CAP 3.9). A price reduction shows the lowest price of the previous 30 days (Directive 98/6/EC Art. 6a). A legal obligation is not sold as a feature (Annex I point 10a, CAP 3.12). Rules: MKT-CLAIM-04, 14, 20, 21, 27, 28, 29.

### Documented gaps

These are findings of the wave-2 follow-ups. They are not open questions: each names what is missing, and the rule severities below already allow for them.

- **Part 465 and business-to-business testimonials** ([followup-q3](marketing-claims-evidence/followup-q3.md), answered partly). Settled: an endorsement by an entity is outside every Part 465 testimonial section, and 465.6 is the only section that reaches entities ([Federal Register, 2024-08-22](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials); [FTC Q&A, November 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). 465.8(b) reaches fake influence indicators used "for a commercial purpose", so it reaches business buyers. Inferred only: a named individual's quote about a product used at work is a consumer testimonial under 465.1(f). No FTC statement, Q&A entry or case says so. The rule text, the 400,590-byte preamble, the Q&A page and two web searches have no B2B passage, and no legal database was searched (it needs a login). The Q&A was read through a fetch tool only, because `curl` was blocked. MKT-CLAIM-20 stays MUST for both kinds of speaker, because Section 5 and 16 CFR 255.1 and 255.4 reach an organisation's endorsement.
- **Directive 98/6/EC Art. 6a and software subscriptions** ([followup-q1](marketing-claims-evidence/followup-q1.md)). The Directive defines no "product", so the text does not say whether a SaaS "was 20 EUR, now 15 EUR" banner is covered. No national act and no CJEU ruling was read, and the owner's seller country is unknown. A false "was" price on a subscription is still attackable under UCPD Art. 6(1)(d). MKT-CLAIM-27 stays SHOULD.
- **UK reach** ([followup-q2](marketing-claims-evidence/followup-q2.md)). Not determined: whether DMCC Part 3 reaches an overseas trader, whether "turnover" in s.182 is global (the CMA says so, the section text does not), whether the CAP Code covers B2B developer-tool claims, whether DMCC names AI-generated reviews expressly, and what the CMA means by "reasonable and proportionate steps". Settled: a paragraph 13 breach is not a s.237 criminal offence (s.237(8)(b)).
- **Pattern recall** ([followup-q0](marketing-claims-evidence/followup-q0.md)). The recall figures come from one reader and the same 26 pages. A held-out set and a second rater are missing. Flipping every borderline call moves union precision between 88.0 % and 91.1 %.
- **Chart method links, MKT-CLAIM-06** ([followup-q0](marketing-claims-evidence/followup-q0.md)). On 124 chart and table values in the cached exemplar HTML, the 300-character link rule flags 105. Bun's values are among them, although Bun links a reproduction page from a header block beside the chart. Three structural wordings were tested (caption link, K ancestors, value-density groups); they pass 19, 41 and 18 values, and none separates "one link covers the chart" from "no link" without depending on one site's layout. A per-chart record is the untested candidate. The rule stays MUST: its false fails are the safe direction, and 27 Deno values have no method link at all.
- **Comparative-advertising case law** ([followup-q4](marketing-claims-evidence/followup-q4.md)). The CJEU texts on "identifies a competitor" and "same needs" (C-381/05, C-159/09) were not fetched. No source says whether a free, non-commercial open-source project is a "competitor". No US case was found on a comparison cell that was true when written and false when read; the expiry in MKT-CLAIM-05 is a design choice.

## The ruleset

Severity: **MUST** blocks publication. **SHOULD** warns, and the warning needs a recorded reason to pass. **CONSIDER** is advice. "Record" means a `[[claim]]` row in the repository-root `claims.toml` with the fields in MKT-CLAIM-04. "The lint" means the claims check described in MKT-CLAIM-01. It does not exist yet and is a new commitment (see "Applied").

**The lint's five checks.** Every verify below that names a check means one of these. They adapt the lint specification of the record dive (claims-evidence-record, CLAIM-12) to this ruleset's single root file.

| Check | What it does | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| A, schema | Reads every `[[claim]]` row against the field tables in MKT-CLAIM-04. | A required field for the row's `class` is missing or empty; `id` is not unique; `observed` is not an ISO date; `refresh_by` is neither an ISO date after `observed` nor the string `"next-release"`; a `method` raw-data path or a `licence` `check` path fails `test -e`. For `endorsement`: `contact_ref` or `permission_ref` is empty; `contact_ref` holds an `@` or 7 or more digits (contact details, not a pointer); a badge row's `permission_ref` is not `<party>: <pointer>`, or its non-URL pointer fails `test -e`; `verification_method` is empty while the row's surface uses a MKT-CLAIM-20 "real customer" word. For `comparison`: `compared_party`, `compared_kind`, `third_party_as_of` or `third_party_version` is missing, or `third_party_as_of` is more than 7 days after `observed`. |
| B, binding | Resolves every `claim:<id>` marker to a row, and every row to its surfaces. | A marker names no row; a row's `claim` string is not byte-equal in a file listed in its `surface`; for `github:description`, the string is absent from `gh repo view <owner>/<repo> --json description -q .description`. |
| C, detection | Runs the MKT-CLAIM-02 pattern set on every added line of a copy surface (code fences and `<style>` blocks removed). | A hit is not covered. A hit is covered by a `claim:<id>` marker at the end of the hit line, or by a line that holds only a marker directly above it, or (for JSON and remote surfaces, which cannot carry a marker) by a live row whose `surface` lists the file and whose `claim` string is on the hit line. An `[[allow]]` table entry with `file`, `text` and `reason` also covers a hit that is not a claim at all ("Quick start" style), except in check E's classes. |
| D, freshness | Compares each row's `refresh_by` with today and with the product version. | `refresh_by` is a date before today, or it is `"next-release"` and the version in the repository's manifest differs from `subject_version`. An expired row counts as absent for checks B, C and E. |
| E, blocked classes | Re-checks the hits of the count, price-or-limit, attestation, AI-capability and endorsement arms: lines 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 12, 13 and 14 of MKT-CLAIM-02. Exceptions: a line-12 hit that matches `[0-9]+\.[0-9]+\+` (a version floor) is dropped first. A line-9 hit whose only match is `agentic` or `intelligent` may take an `[[allow]]` entry (C15). A line-13 hit whose only match is a `free` phrase is also covered by a live `licence` row when the same sentence names that row's SPDX id (the licence route of MKT-CLAIM-13). | Such a hit is covered only by an `[[allow]]` entry, or by no live row of the matching class. For `class = "count"`, the lint re-runs `check` and fails when its output differs from `value`. A `performance`, `comparison`, `statistic`, `count` or `ai-capability` row whose `source` is an endorsement fails (MKT-CLAIM-28). |

Markers, by surface:

| Surface | Marker |
|---|---|
| Markdown, HTML, Astro templates | `<!-- claim:<id> -->` |
| MDX (`.mdx`): "Instead of HTML comments, you can use JavaScript comments in braces" ([mdxjs.com, What is MDX](https://mdxjs.com/docs/what-is-mdx/), page undated, read 2026-11-27) | `{/* claim:<id> */}` |
| TypeScript, JavaScript | `// claim:<id>` |
| TOML (`Cargo.toml`, `pyproject.toml`, `claims.toml`), YAML frontmatter | `# claim:<id>` |
| JSON (`package.json`, extension manifests), `github:description` | no marker; covered only by the `surface` binding |

Each marker sits at the end of the hit line, or alone on the line directly above it. A marker at the end of one line never covers the next line.

### Gate and record

**MKT-CLAIM-01 (MUST).** Run the claims lint in CI on every change to a copy surface, before publication. A pattern hit (MKT-CLAIM-02) on an added line must be covered by a marker that resolves to a live record (marker table above), or by the record's `surface` binding, or render its value from that record. Without a record, rewrite the sentence (MKT-CLAIM-08) or delete it.
- *Rationale:* the basis must exist before dissemination ([FTC 1984](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation)), and no human reviews the agent's output.
- *Verify:* checks A to E pass in CI. The marker half of check C, as a reference shell function (tested 2026-11-27 on a Markdown and a TOML fixture: it passed a marker-only line above and an end-of-line marker, and failed a hit whose line above carried another line's marker):
  ```sh
  # usage: git diff -U0 origin/main -- "$f" | unmarked "$f"   (prints one line per uncovered hit)
  unmarked() {
    awk '/^@@/{split($3,a,/[+,]/);n=a[2];next} /^\+\+\+/{next} /^\+/{print n++}' | while read -r n; do
      sed -n "${n}p" "$1" | grep -q -i -E -f claim-patterns.txt || continue
      sed -n "${n}p" "$1" | grep -q -E 'claim:[a-z0-9-]+' && continue
      [ "$n" -gt 1 ] && sed -n "$((n-1))p" "$1" | grep -q -E '^[[:space:]]*(<!--|//|#|\{/\*)[[:space:]]*claim:[a-z0-9-]+[[:space:]]*(-->|\*/\})?[[:space:]]*$' && continue
      echo "$1:$n: claim pattern without a claim:<id> marker"
    done; }
  ```

|---|---|
| performance, comparison | 90 days, or the next minor release of the subject or any compared product, whichever comes first |

### Endorsements and proof

**MKT-CLAIM-20 (MUST).** Never write a quote, a name with a role, a rating, a review, a testimonial, a customer logo, a review-site badge or an adopter name unless an `endorsement` record backs it. Never generate one, never write a composite, and never draft review text for a customer to sign. A blank list of questions is allowed. A persona appears only under a heading labelled "Persona", without quotation marks. An adopter name needs a self-added pull request or a written permission. Dependency graphs, lockfiles and download counts are evidence of use, not consent. If the page states or implies that reviews come from people who used or bought the product ("verified", "real customers", "from users"), the same page states whether and how that is checked, and the row holds `verification_method`. Never write that reviews come from real customers without one. A badge row (`kind = "badge"`) holds `permission_ref` in the form `<authorising party>: <pointer>`, where the pointer is a URL or a repository path that exists.
- *Rationale:* 465.2(a) bars writing or creating a consumer testimonial that misrepresents the individual's existence or experience ([eCFR Part 465](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465), in force 2024-10-21). It reaches quotes from individuals, and by this topic's inference also individuals who use the product at work. It does not reach quotes, logos or adopter names credited to an organisation. The FTC places those under the Endorsement Guides ([Federal Register, 2024-08-22](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials)) and Section 5. 255.4(a) requires an organisation's endorsement to reflect "the collective judgment of the organization" ([eCFR Part 255](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255), point-in-time 2026-11-01; followup-q3). The agent must not invent either kind. "AI-generated reviews are covered by the final rule" ([Federal Register, 2024-08-22, fn. 35](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials)). "The name or seal of an organization can be endorsements" (255.0(b)). In the EU, Annex I point 23b makes it unfair in all circumstances to state that reviews come from consumers who used or bought the product "without taking reasonable and proportionate steps to check". Point 23c bars submitting or commissioning false reviews or endorsements and "misrepresenting consumer reviews or social endorsements" ([UCPD consolidated 2026-09-27](http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/02005L0029-20260927), fetched 2026-11-27; B2C only, Art. 3(1)). In the UK, DMCC Act 2024 Schedule 20 paragraph 13 bars a review that "purports to be, but is not, based on a person's genuine experience" (in force 2025-04-06), and CAP 3.52 bars a "trust mark, quality mark or equivalent without the necessary authorisation" ([Schedule 20](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20); [CAP Code s.3](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html), both read 2026-11-27; followup-q2).
- *Verify:* every `<blockquote>`, every `[“"][^”"]{20,}[”"]\s*[-—–]\s*[A-Z]` match, every `<img alt="...logo...">` inside a proof block, every badge image and every `ADOPTERS.md` entry carries `data-proof="<id>"` or a `claim:` marker that resolves to an `endorsement` row whose `permission.type` is `written` or `public-statement`. So does every line matching `^@[A-Za-z0-9_]{2,}$` or `@[A-Za-z0-9_]{2,} on X$`, the author handle of a social-post testimonial. On exemplars that pattern found 21 author lines, where the `<blockquote>` pattern found 1; on the fleet it found 0 (followup-q0). Every hit of `verified (review|customer|purchase)|real (customers|users)|from (our )?(customers|users)` resolves to an `endorsement` row with a non-empty `verification_method`, and the same page carries a sentence matching `checked|verified by|we (confirm|verify)`. Measured 2026-11-27: 0 fleet lines, and 1 exemplar line, a false hit ("signals from users and AI agents", PostHog). Badge rows pass check A (`lint_q2.py 20` in followup-q2).

## AI-agent failure modes

Ranked by how often each bites, grouped by mechanism. "Check" names the rule that catches it.

**1. Filling a slot with plausible content (most frequent).** Asked for a hero, a pricing page or a proof section, the agent produces content shaped like the exemplars.
- It invents a number: "3x faster", "50% smaller", "10,000+ developers" (02, 03).
- It invents a price, plan name or free tier for a product that has none (03).
- It invents a testimonial, a persona with a name and role, or a "Trusted by teams at Google, Stripe" line from memory or from a dependency graph (20, 24).
- It quotes a remembered statistic ("78% of developers", 95:5) (07).
- It writes "verified reviews from real customers" over quotes that nobody checked (20).
- It backs a number with a customer quote (28).
- It fills a comparison card with a competitor limitation from memory ("nvm: no Windows") (30).

**2. Training-data vocabulary.** The agent writes what the corpus writes.
- "Blazing fast", "lightning-fast install", "instantly" (02, 08).
- "SOC 2 certified" or "GDPR compliant", copied from Tailscale and 1Password style (14).
- "AI-powered" for a repository built with an AI assistant, or for a tool that configures AI agents (18).
- "Secure by default" with no mechanism (08).
- "Agentic" or "intelligent" as a product label (02 line 9, 18).
- Market-standard figures the wave-1 arms could not see: "100k+ users", "1.76s", "Up to 10 sites" (02 lines 11 to 13, 03).

**3. Tightening drift.** A rewrite for punch loses the checkable part.
- "Signed releases with SBOM" becomes "secure supply chain" (08: the mechanism token is gone from the hunk).
- "No third-party audit", "early development" or "no timing is claimed" gets deleted as negative (10).
- A customer's words get paraphrased inside quotation marks (21).
- A caveat moves into a tooltip or a `<details>` element (29). "Reviews are not checked" gets deleted (10).

**4. Stale carry-over.**
- A count survives a release ("10 clients" after an 11th client ships) (03, 05).
- A licence is described from pre-relicensing memory (Terraform, Redis and Elastic all changed between 2021 and 2025) (11).
- A deprecated SPDX id (`GPL-3.0`, `MIT/Apache-2.0`) gets copied into a manifest (12).
- The checker reads only Cargo's `[package]` and reports "no licence" for a workspace (12).
- SPDX id `Fair` gets read as "fair source" (11).

**5. Exemplar imitation.**
- An undated, unlinked quote wall: 0 of 8 exemplar pages dated their quotes (21).
- "Case study" over a story with a percentage that has no base (23).
- A vendor benchmark chart reproduced without its hardware or version (06, 07).

**6. Scope errors.**
- "Free" in the hero with the paid tier below the fold (13).
- "Open source" for an open-core product (11).
- "Every coding agent" next to a list of 10 (02, the universal arm).
- "Compliant" for one component, read as the whole service (14).
- A legal obligation sold as a feature ("we encrypt personal data as required by law") (14).
- A struck-through "was $20" anchored on a list price (27).

**7. Gaming the gate.** An agent blocked by the lint may add a `claim:` marker with no record, an `allow` entry for a price, or a record it filled itself for a sign-off class. Markers must resolve (01). Blocked classes have no `allow` (03). Sign-off rows need an approving review from a GitHub login that authored neither the pull request nor its commits; a typed `approved_by`, a commit author name or a commit signature proves nothing (16). An agent may also try to lengthen a `refresh_by` interval; check D rejects that (05).

## What an agent must ask a human

- Any price, plan, tier, limit, quota, discount or free-tier boundary, and the prior price of any reduction announcement (MKT-CLAIM-27). The owner's answer becomes the record's `source`.
- Whether to name a competitor or benchmark against one, and every addition, removal or re-ordering of a compared party (MKT-CLAIM-16, 30). The ocx drift table already names apt, brew, winget, nvm and setup-node. Whether a non-commercial open-source project counts as a "competitor" is unsettled.
- Whether a surface is aimed at UK or EU consumers rather than at developers acting for their business, the seller's country (for 98/6/EC Art. 6a), and, if a review or rating feed is ever added, who runs the "reasonable and proportionate steps" process of DMCC Schedule 20 paragraph 13(3).
- Whether a sentence that names a legal duty presents it as a distinguishing feature (MKT-CLAIM-14, Annex I point 10a).
- Whether the organisation holds a SOC report or an ISO certificate, and its period, examiner, scope and access route.
- Any statement that a product is compliant with a law (GDPR, HIPAA, CCPA, EU AI Act), and the Art. 50 position for an AI feature or for agent-written public text.
- Any response-time, SLA, uptime, support or bounty promise, including the existing `SECURITY.md` "48 hours" line.
- Any AI accuracy figure, comparison or "replaces" claim, and whether a feature truly calls a model when the call site is not obvious.
- Choosing, changing or adding a licence, including adding a LICENSE file to the four repositories that lack one. Using the OSI logo or the "OSI Approved" mark.
- Every endorsement: asking for permission (the owner sends the request), accepting a quote from an employee, founder, investor or relative, deciding whether a connection is "reasonably expected" by the audience, displaying a third-party logo or badge whose licence terms the agent could not read, offering a review incentive, and naming an adopter found only in public data.
- Whether a referent-free tagline ("The Simple Package Manager") stays.
- Whether to call a product "stable", "production-ready" or "1.0". That is a release decision.
- Whether a law row the agent re-fetched still applies. The 6-month refresh is a fetch, not a legal opinion.
