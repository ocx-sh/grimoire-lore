# Voice and AI authorship

The question: how does an agent write copy in a project's voice that readers do not reject as AI slop, and when must AI involvement be disclosed?

## Verdict

1. **Readers punish content, not punctuation, so the rules aim at content first.** In 284 HN comments from 2026 that call a README or site AI-written, 57 infer the author did not care, 21 ask for the author's own words, 18 object to vague "so what" claims, 13 to length, and only 6 name the em dash ([tells-baseline §8](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/voice-ai-tells-baseline.md#8-reader-reported-tells-in-2026-hacker-news-comments)). These counts show where the stake is. They do not by themselves justify a ban: MKT-VOICE-06 and -17 rest on the fact that an invented experience or a fabricated demo is a false statement, and the comments only rank them first. Claims with no evidence are already MKT-CLAIM-02, -06, -08 and -09, and this topic does not restate them.
2. **No detector and no style marker ever gates.** Both dives agree. The favourable 2026 detector studies (Epoch AI 2026-07-15, 0 of 495 false positives for Pangram and GPTZero; NBER w34223, September 2025) tested long plain prose, not short, list-heavy, non-native or tool-edited README and landing copy. Vendors also update silently, so a CI result cannot be reproduced next quarter. We go further than "advisory": no detector runs in CI at all, and an agent never cites a detector score (MKT-VOICE-01).
3. **Conflict 1, the em dash: ban or density warning? Resolved by splitting on who writes the line, and by deferring to DOC-PLAIN-01 wherever it already runs.** tells-baseline proposed a density warning at 3.0 per 1,000 words on marketing surfaces (VAI-03). voice-guide-encoding said it is a punctuation rule, not a sign of authorship, and Wikipedia now files it as a "historical" tell. Both are right about different text. For **lines the agent adds**, the rule is zero em dashes (MKT-VOICE-03). Twelve instruction-tuned models emit 0.00 to 10.62 per 1,000 words when unconstrained (Freeburg, [arXiv 2603.27006](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27006) Table 1, 2026-03-27, fetched 2026-11-27). An explicit "Do not use em dashes." instruction lowers the rate, but compliance varies by model. In the paper's Table 2 it gives Claude Opus 4.6 8.46 to 0.00, GPT-5.4 0.75 to 0.00, DeepSeek V3 8.66 to 1.57 and GPT-4.1 11.51 to 3.86. Only those four models were tested with the prohibition. So the instruction is not the gate. The gate is the diff grep in MKT-VOICE-03, which needs no discrimination power and so does not decay when the tell does. For **existing text**, whose author is unknown, the density figure is a report only, never a rewrite trigger (MKT-VOICE-04). Where a repository runs DOC-PLAIN-01, that rule already bans em dashes in every file its checker reads, including docs-site home pages, with a changed-lines rollout. There DOC-PLAIN-01 wins, and MKT-VOICE-03 and -04 apply only to copy its checker does not read. This keeps DOC-PLAIN-09 intact: no rule here claims anything about who wrote a page.
4. **Only one of 35 markers survived a pre-registered baseline, so tell lists need a measured baseline before they ship.** Triads, emoji, bold-lead bullets, "navigate" and "ensure" all occur in 2022 human READMEs at rates that would flag human writers. That admission criterion governs catalog authors, not a copy-writing agent, so it is now a catalog-maintenance note below rather than a rule (MKT-VOICE-05 retired). Vocabulary tells stay a density measure under DOC-PLAIN-10.
5. **Conflict 2, a new word table or the positioning record? Resolved for the record.** voice-guide-encoding proposed an 11-column `words.tsv`, a `voice_words.py` script and generated Vale files. Three measurements and one sibling decision overrule it. DOC-PLAIN-12 fires 0 true positives on 6 marketing surfaces and 460,690 docs words. Claim words (fast, instant, secure) already belong to MKT-CLAIM-02 and -09. MKT-POS-13 already holds product-specific bans as `do_not_say` entries with `pattern`, `reason`, `instead` and `lift_when`, which is the encoding shape the dive asked for, minus Vale. We therefore build no third word list and no Vale generation (MKT-VOICE-10). The useful part of the dive survives: every ban carries a positive replacement, and the guidance shows the replacement first.
6. **The voice itself lives in a small per-project voice file, owned by a human, in the same status pattern as the positioning record.** A principle without example copy cannot be checked: Mailchimp and Atlassian publish none on their voice pages, while Monzo's surface matrix can be looked up. The file holds one pass and one fail sentence per principle, plus a register row per surface. The agent drafts the file when it is missing and never marks it reviewed (MKT-VOICE-08).
7. **Conflict 3, a human-written lead (VAI-11) or an agent-drafted record (MKT-POS-03)? Resolved by narrowing the ban to what only a person can know.** The positioning sentence stays agent-drafted under MKT-POS. What the agent may never invent is a first-person account of the author's past: motive, experience, usage history or an origin story. It leaves `ASK-OWNER` in place of that sentence (MKT-VOICE-06). The MUST rests on fabrication: the agent has no source for a fact about a person's past, and no claim record can cover one. The SHOULD-level "human writes the lead" shrinks to that ban, which a past-tense pattern and a resolvable owner quote can check.
8. **Ordinary marketing text needs no AI disclosure, and no page carries an authorship statement in either direction.** The Commission guidelines (C(2026) 7702, dated 2026-11-05) put advertising and product-description text outside Art. 50(4) unless it makes health, safety or sustainability claims ([disclosure §2.3](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/voice-ai-disclosure.md#23-text)). Neither "AI-assisted" nor "human-written" appears on a page (MKT-VOICE-14). Text about a product's own AI features is not an authorship statement, so the check matches only phrasings about who wrote the page. Commit trailers are not page content: DOC-PLAIN-22 governs them for documentation changes, and this topic sets no trailer rule. A voluntary public AI-use statement is an owner decision.
9. **Conflict 4, classify every agent text in the PR body (VAD-02), or classify by topic? Resolved for topic.** Recording "Art. 50(4) scope: out" on every README typo fix is noise. Only text on a listed public-interest topic triggers the duty: security advisories, incident posts, and health, environment, consumer-safety and financial explainers. That text gets named human fact-check review, recorded as an approving pull-request review on the head commit, and no agent edit follows it (MKT-VOICE-15). The mechanical trigger is an advisory identifier or a notice title, not a keyword such as "vulnerability", which ordinary docs about security features use.
10. **Media is where the legal duty bites.** A generated image, video or audio that could pass as a real product, person or event, including a generated screenshot shown as the real UI, needs a visible label at first exposure. Metadata never counts as that label (MKT-VOICE-16). The label wording is chosen so it does not trip DOC-PLAIN-08's badge pattern. `Generated with AI` and `Edited with AI` do not match `CHATBOT_RE` in `prose.py:67-74`, and `AI-generated` does (tested 2026-11-27). So the label needs no change to the checker, which resolves the DOC-PLAIN-08 conflict the disclosure dive left open. Keeping C2PA metadata through a build is practical, not a deployer's legal duty: the Code's non-removal commitment binds signatory providers ([disclosure VAD-13](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/voice-ai-disclosure.md)), so MKT-VOICE-18 is CONSIDER.
11. **The scout's 2026-12-02 date does not apply to adopters.** It ends the providers' Art. 50(2) marking transition only. Deployer labelling has applied since 2026-08-02 (guidelines para 153). This is recorded under Decay rather than as a rule, because it stops mattering five days after this file.
12. **Documented gap: no in-repository check can tell an agent from the owner it commits as.** `git log --format=%an` shows a git identity, and the dive found 2 AI trailer lines in 177 fleet README commits, so agent commits usually carry the owner's name. MKT-VOICE-08 and -15 therefore check a pull-request approval event instead, where GitHub refuses self-approval, so the approver is a second account. A solo-owner repository cannot produce that event. There the rules are instructions without a mechanical check: the agent never flips `reviewed` and never publishes in-scope text, and the owner does both by hand. MKT-VOICE-06's owner-quote check has the same limit against an agent that posts comments with the owner's token. This matches MKT-POS-04's Verdict 11.

## The ruleset

Severity is for an agent acting with no human in the loop. "Copy surface" means the set MKT-CLAIM defines: READMEs, pitch and landing pages, docs-site home pages, page titles and meta descriptions, manifest descriptions, release notes, email, and the string literals and JSX text that render on those pages. A surface is read from `doc_surface` (MKT-PAGE), never from its path. `<copy paths>` is the repository's list of those files. `$DQ` is the directory where the docs-quality-reference skill's checks are installed: `skills/docs-quality-reference/checks` in this catalog, and `.claude/rules/docs-quality/checks` in `ocx-sh/catalog` (`ls /home/mherwig/dev/ocx-sh/catalog/.claude/rules/docs-quality/checks`, 2026-11-27). Commands marked "block" are in [Check blocks](#check-blocks) below the table.

## AI-agent failure modes

Ranked by how often each bites, grouped by mechanism.

1. **Default model style leaks into copy (every unconstrained draft).** The agent writes spaced em dashes, long rule-voiced sentences, "never" and "every", and no "you". An instruction against the dash helps some models fully and others only partly (GPT-4.1 still 3.86 per 1,000 under an explicit prohibition). Measured: the fleet's 13.51 em dashes per 1,000 words, 16.9-word sentences and 0.42 contractions per 1,000. Caught by MKT-VOICE-03 (or DOC-PLAIN-01 where it runs) and -09.
2. **Habitual authorship marking (observed in the fleet).** The agent adds "Generated with Claude" footers to pages (MKT-VOICE-14) or `Co-Authored-By: Claude` trailers to documentation commits, as in [ocx-sh/website@0477a6b](https://github.com/ocx-sh/website/commit/0477a6b3f26e40d7517cddde113864073714606e) (DOC-PLAIN-22).
3. **Fabricated authenticity.** The agent invents a motive, a production history or "we got tired of X". It writes a terminal transcript instead of recording one, renders a mockup as the product, or adds a "human-written" badge. Caught by MKT-VOICE-06, -17, -16 and -14.
4. **Checker misuse.** The agent runs `prose.py` on `.astro` or `.ts` and "fixes" TypeScript semicolons. It reads the page type from a path, or strips the pitch from a pitch page because docs-style says "no marketing open". Caught by MKT-VOICE-11 and -12.
5. **Treating tells as a verdict.** The agent cites a detector percentage, strips "ensure", "navigate" or emoji from human text, rewrites a non-native author's voice, or pushes text through a humanizer. Caught by MKT-VOICE-01, -02 and -04, and by DOC-PLAIN-09 and -10.
6. **Wrong disclosure model.** The agent treats Art. 50 as a blanket duty to label all AI text, or as no duty at all. It treats 2026-12-02 as its own deadline, counts its own or a second model's review as human review, re-polishes text after sign-off, embeds C2PA data and calls that the label, or signs with the test certificate. Caught by MKT-VOICE-15, -16 and -18.
7. **Self-attestation.** The agent writes its own `Owner words:` or `Editorial review:` line, or commits `status = "reviewed"` under the owner's git identity. Caught by the forge checks in MKT-VOICE-06, -08 and -15, except in a solo-owner repository (Verdict 12).
8. **Word-list sprawl.** The agent starts a new banned list, swaps "leverage" for an equally empty "harness", or writes "do not use X" and primes the word. Caught by MKT-VOICE-10.
9. **Wrong tone in failure notices.** The agent writes a cheerful exclamation in an incident note or apologises three times. Caught by MKT-VOICE-13.

## What an agent must ask a human

- The voice file's principles, pass and fail sentences, register per surface, and its `reviewed` status (MKT-VOICE-08).
- The owner's own words for any motive, experience or origin sentence, posted where the forge records the owner as author (MKT-VOICE-06).
- Whether to rewrite existing text that a marker flags, above all text by a non-native author (MKT-VOICE-04).
- Any voluntary public statement about AI use, either "this README was written by a human" or a Slack-style "we disclose AI use" ([Slack](https://slack.design/articles/slack-content-design-in-the-age-of-ai/)).
- Who holds editorial responsibility, and whether to publish that contact, given the deliberate no-Impressum line at `privacy.html:47` (MKT-VOICE-15).
- Whether a borderline artefact counts as public interest: a security advisory, a compliance explainer, a sustainability line.
- Publishing AI imagery of a real person, customer or lookalike. That also needs consent, and endorsements belong to MKT-CLAIM-20.
- Buying a C2PA signing certificate and holding its key. Adopting the EU icon or a house label wording.
- Whether shipping a generative feature makes the project a provider under Art. 50(1) and (2).
- Posting agent-drafted text to a venue that forbids it. The HN guidelines say "Don't post generated text or AI-edited text" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html), read 2026-11-27). The channels sub-program owns venue rules.

- Do US state laws (the California AI Transparency Act as amended, and others) or UK rules oblige a software marketer outside the EU to label AI-generated text or media on its own site? The disclosure dive covered EU law only, and the California page returned HTTP 403.
- Does a pre-LLM baseline for landing pages, release notes and email, not only READMEs, support the em dash and register markers? Which markers separate late-2026 model output now that em dashes can be prompted away in some models? The baseline is 36 README documents and fits its threshold to them.
- Does a reader test show that copy which follows MKT-VOICE-06 and -07 is rejected less often than an unconstrained agent draft? Every piece of reader evidence here is an accusatory HN comment, which is a convenience sample, not an outcome measure.
- Has any national market-surveillance authority, or a Commission Q&A, said whether a software security advisory or incident post is "public interest" text under Art. 50(4)? The guidelines list "public security" and "consumer safety" but give no software example.
- What do published incident, outage and security notices from software vendors measure for exclamation marks, apology count and a named next step? MKT-VOICE-13 stays CONSIDER until such a corpus is measured.

Owner decisions:

- Adopt `.agents/voice.toml` for ocx and Grimoire, and choose the pitch register: second person and contractions, or the current impersonal register.
- Approve or decline removing the em dashes in the existing pitch copy at `index.astro:38,46,79` and in `grimoire-rs/grimoire/README.md`.
- Scope the three docs-style "no marketing open" lines (MKT-VOICE-12), which are edits to fleet config this program may not make.
- Whether to name an editorial-responsibility contact, or to keep the no-Impressum stance and never publish agent-drafted in-scope text.
- For a solo-owner repository, accept that MKT-VOICE-08 and -15 have no mechanical check, or add a second reviewer account (Verdict 12).
- DOC-PLAIN-22 bans tool co-author trailers on documentation commits, while an agent harness may be told to add attribution lines to every commit. Decide which wins per change type. That is docs-quality's rule, not this topic's.
