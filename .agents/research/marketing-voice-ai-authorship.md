# Voice and AI authorship

The question: how does an agent write copy in a project's voice that readers do not reject as AI slop, and when must AI involvement be disclosed?

## Verdict

1. **Readers punish content, not punctuation, so the rules aim at content first.** In 284 HN comments from 2026 that call a README or site AI-written, 57 infer the author did not care, 21 ask for the author's own words, 18 object to vague "so what" claims, 13 to length, and only 6 name the em dash ([tells-baseline §8](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/voice-ai-tells-baseline.md#8-reader-reported-tells-in-2026-hacker-news-comments)). These counts show where the stake is. They do not by themselves justify a ban: MKT-VOICE-06 and -17 rest on the fact that an invented experience or a fabricated demo is a false statement, and the comments only rank them first. Claims with no evidence are already MKT-CLAIM-02, -06, -08 and -09, and this topic does not restate them.
2. **No detector and no style marker ever gates.** Both dives agree. The favourable 2026 detector studies (Epoch AI 2026-07-15, 0 of 495 false positives for Pangram and GPTZero; NBER w34223, September 2025) tested long plain prose, not short, list-heavy, non-native or tool-edited README and landing copy. Vendors also update silently, so a CI result cannot be reproduced next quarter. Wave 2 adds two more reasons. Style-feature detection loses 20.42 percent F1 on average under subtle persuasive rewriting (Persuaficial, [arXiv 2601.04925v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04925v2), 2026-04-21, news, X and Reddit text, not marketing copy). And the tell count barely moves between model versions while single tells vanish ([followup-q1 §6](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/followup-q1.md#6-which-markers-separate-late-2026-output)). We go further than "advisory": no detector runs in CI at all, and an agent never cites a detector score (MKT-VOICE-01).
3. **Conflict 1, the em dash: ban or density warning? Resolved by splitting on who writes the line, and by deferring to DOC-PLAIN-01 wherever it already runs.** tells-baseline proposed a density warning at 3.0 per 1,000 words on marketing surfaces (VAI-03). voice-guide-encoding said it is a punctuation rule, not a sign of authorship, and Wikipedia now files it as a "historical" tell, dated "2022 to September 2026". Both are right about different text. For **lines the agent adds**, the rule is zero em dashes (MKT-VOICE-03). Twelve instruction-tuned models emit 0.00 to 10.62 per 1,000 words when unconstrained (Freeburg, [arXiv 2603.27006](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27006) Table 1, 2026-03-27, fetched 2026-11-27). An explicit "Do not use em dashes." instruction lowers the rate, but compliance varies by model. In the paper's Table 2 it gives Claude Opus 4.6 8.46 to 0.00, GPT-5.4 0.75 to 0.00, DeepSeek V3 8.66 to 1.57 and GPT-4.1 11.51 to 3.86. Only those four models were tested with the prohibition. Late-2026 models drop the dash without being told: Graphite measures Opus 5.5 at 0.015 per 1,000 words against 2.92 for Opus 5 on a prompt that does not mention it ([Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/research/ai-tells-opus-5-5-update), 2026-10-01, vendor, fetched 2026-11-27). So the instruction is not the gate, and the dash is no longer evidence of authorship either way. The gate is the diff grep in MKT-VOICE-03, which needs no discrimination power and so does not decay when the tell does. For **existing text**, whose author is unknown, the density figure is a report only, never a rewrite trigger (MKT-VOICE-04). It now needs 6 or more dashes as well as 3.0 per 1,000 words, because 2 of 18 pre-2022 landing pages reach 3.0 with 3 dashes each, while no baseline document holds more than 5 ([followup-q1 §2](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/followup-q1.md#2-landing-pages-pre-llm-snapshot-closest-to-2022-09-01)). Where a repository runs DOC-PLAIN-01, that rule already bans em dashes in every file its checker reads, including docs-site home pages, with a changed-lines rollout. There DOC-PLAIN-01 wins, and MKT-VOICE-03 and -04 apply only to copy its checker does not read. This keeps DOC-PLAIN-09 intact: no rule here claims anything about who wrote a page.
4. **Only one of 35 markers survived a pre-registered baseline, so tell lists need a measured baseline before they ship.** Triads, emoji, bold-lead bullets, "navigate" and "ensure" all occur in 2022 human READMEs at rates that would flag human writers. That admission criterion governs catalog authors, not a copy-writing agent, so it is now a catalog-maintenance note below rather than a rule (MKT-VOICE-05 retired). Vocabulary tells stay a density measure under DOC-PLAIN-10. What still separates late-2026 output sits at phrase and frame level: corrective framing and importance flags ("is more than a", "rather than simply", "why X matters"). Graphite counts 2,548 tells for Opus 5.5 against 2,666 for Opus 5, "just 4% fewer" (vendor study of web articles). Six such patterns hit 0 lines in 16,243 words of 2022 landing pages ([followup-q1 §7](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/followup-q1.md#7-a-runnable-check-for-the-phrase-tells)). So the agent keeps them out of the lines it adds (MKT-VOICE-20, CONSIDER), as house style, and never uses them to judge existing text.
5. **Conflict 2, a new word table or the positioning record? Resolved for the record.** voice-guide-encoding proposed an 11-column `words.tsv`, a `voice_words.py` script and generated Vale files. Three measurements and one sibling decision overrule it. DOC-PLAIN-12 fires 0 true positives on 6 marketing surfaces and 460,690 docs words. Claim words (fast, instant, secure) already belong to MKT-CLAIM-02 and -09. MKT-POS-13 already holds product-specific bans as `do_not_say` entries with `pattern`, `reason`, `instead` and `lift_when`, which is the encoding shape the dive asked for, minus Vale. We therefore build no third word list and no Vale generation (MKT-VOICE-10). The useful part of the dive survives: every ban carries a positive replacement, and the guidance shows the replacement first.
6. **The voice itself lives in a small per-project voice file, owned by a human, in the same status pattern as the positioning record.** A principle without example copy cannot be checked: Mailchimp and Atlassian publish none on their voice pages, while Monzo's surface matrix can be looked up. The file holds one pass and one fail sentence per principle, plus a register row per surface. The agent drafts the file when it is missing and never marks it reviewed (MKT-VOICE-08).
7. **Conflict 3, a human-written lead (VAI-11) or an agent-drafted record (MKT-POS-03)? Resolved by narrowing the ban to what only a person can know.** The positioning sentence stays agent-drafted under MKT-POS. What the agent may never invent is a first-person account of the author's past: motive, experience, usage history or an origin story. It leaves `ASK-OWNER` in place of that sentence (MKT-VOICE-06). The MUST rests on fabrication: the agent has no source for a fact about a person's past, and no claim record can cover one. The SHOULD-level "human writes the lead" shrinks to that ban, which a past-tense pattern and a resolvable owner quote can check.
8. **Ordinary marketing text needs no AI disclosure, and no page carries an authorship statement in either direction.** The Commission guidelines (C(2026) 7702, dated 2026-11-05) put advertising and product-description text outside Art. 50(4) unless it makes health, safety or sustainability claims ([disclosure §2.3](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/voice-ai-disclosure.md#23-text)). US state and UK law do not change this for text. Pennsylvania Act 99 exempts content "limited to text" (Section 4(b)(3)), California SB 1050 reaches only an "audio, video, or audiovisual message", New York GBL 396-b only an "audiovisual and/or visual performance", and the ASA states "There is no blanket legal requirement in the UK to disclose the use of AI in ads" (2025-05-29) ([followup-q2](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/followup-q2.md)). The California AI Transparency Act binds the maker of a generative system, not a publisher of its output. Neither "AI-assisted" nor "human-written" appears on a page (MKT-VOICE-14). Text about a product's own AI features is not an authorship statement, so the check matches only phrasings about who wrote the page. Commit trailers are not page content: DOC-PLAIN-22 governs them for documentation changes, and this topic sets no trailer rule. A voluntary public AI-use statement is an owner decision.
9. **Conflict 4, classify every agent text in the PR body (VAD-02), or classify by topic? Resolved for topic.** Recording "Art. 50(4) scope: out" on every README typo fix is noise. Only text on a listed public-interest topic triggers the duty: security advisories, incident posts, and health, environment, consumer-safety and financial explainers. That text gets named human fact-check review, recorded as an approving pull-request review on the head commit, and no agent edit follows it (MKT-VOICE-15). The mechanical trigger is an advisory identifier or a notice title, not a keyword such as "vulnerability", which ordinary docs about security features use. **Documented gap: no authority has ruled on software advisories.** The guidelines, the Commission Art. 50 FAQ, the AI Act Service Desk FAQ and the German (BNetzA), Spanish (AESIA) and Austrian (RTR) authority texts are silent, and the guidelines text holds 0 matches for `cybersecurity|security advisor|security incident|data breach|CVE-|GHSA-` ([followup-q3](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/followup-q3.md)). The default is in scope, by analogy to the guidelines' weather-warning example ("warning citizens about stormy weather and related precautionary measures"). That is an inference, and the rule says so. The owner may record the opposite. The review exception also needs a publicly findable page that names the person or function with editorial responsibility and gives contact details (para 138), so MKT-VOICE-15 now checks that page. A notice sent only to named customers is not published text (para 131 (i)).
10. **Media is where the legal duty bites.** A generated image, video or audio that could pass as a real product, person or event, including a generated screenshot shown as the real UI, needs a visible label at first exposure. Metadata never counts as that label (MKT-VOICE-16). The label wording is chosen so it does not trip DOC-PLAIN-08's badge pattern. `Generated with AI` and `Edited with AI` do not match `CHATBOT_RE` in `prose.py:67-74`, and `AI-generated` does (tested 2026-11-27). So the label needs no change to the checker, which resolves the DOC-PLAIN-08 conflict the disclosure dive left open. Three US states add an advertising duty for media: New York (synthetic performer, in force 2026-06-09), Pennsylvania (synthetic content that misleads about the goods, in force 2026-12-21) and California SB 1050 (synthetic performer, 2027-01-01 per four law firms). A generated human performer therefore needs the owner's written yes and its own caption inside the same `<figure>` (MKT-VOICE-16). The three statutory captions match neither `CHATBOT_RE` nor `authorship.re` (tested 2026-11-27). Keeping C2PA metadata through a build is practical, not a deployer's legal duty: the Code's non-removal commitment binds signatory providers ([disclosure VAD-13](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/voice-ai-disclosure.md)), so MKT-VOICE-18 is CONSIDER.
11. **The scout's 2026-12-02 date does not apply to adopters.** It ends the providers' Art. 50(2) marking transition only. Deployer labelling has applied since 2026-08-02 (guidelines para 153). This is recorded under Decay rather than as a rule, because it stops mattering five days after this file.
12. **Documented gap: no in-repository check can tell an agent from the owner it commits as.** `git log --format=%an` shows a git identity, and the dive found 2 AI trailer lines in 177 fleet README commits, so agent commits usually carry the owner's name. MKT-VOICE-08 and -15 therefore check a pull-request approval event instead, where GitHub refuses self-approval, so the approver is a second account. A solo-owner repository cannot produce that event. There the rules are instructions without a mechanical check: the agent never flips `reviewed` and never publishes in-scope text, and the owner does both by hand. MKT-VOICE-06's owner-quote check has the same limit against an agent that posts comments with the owner's token. This matches MKT-POS-04's Verdict 11.
13. **Failure notices: no exclamation mark, at most two apologies, and a reader step only when the reader has something to do.** 32 vendor notices (2023-11 to 2026-11) hold 0 exclamation marks outside page chrome. 10 of 13 Cloudflare postmortems apologise exactly twice, once near the start and once near the end, and no notice apologises more than twice. 5 of 5 security and breach notices that ask readers to act name the act with a version, a credential or a link, while 0 of 26 outage postmortems and availability reports ask the reader to do anything ([followup-q0](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/followup-q0.md)). So MKT-VOICE-13 moves to SHOULD with a cap of 2, and its old "name the next step" clause becomes MKT-VOICE-19, limited to security and breach notices. This is vendor practice, not a reader-outcome measure.
14. **Documented gaps from wave 2 (2026-11-27).** (a) No pre-LLM baseline exists here for marketing email or for prose release announcements. No labelled, openly readable email corpus was reachable, and GitHub release bodies need an API token. MKT-VOICE-04 and -09 therefore rest on READMEs, landing pages and changelogs only ([followup-q1 §5](marketing-voice-ai-authorship/followup-q1.md#5-email)). (b) The notice corpus behind MKT-VOICE-13 and -19 is a convenience sample skewed to Cloudflare (13) and GitHub (10). Small-maintainer GitHub Security Advisories, the notices this fleet would write, were not sampled. (c) The chaptered texts of California SB 1000 and SB 1050 returned HTTP 403. So SB 1050's effective date (2027-01-01 per four law firms, "immediately" per one) and SB 1000's covered-provider definition were not read. No guidance says whether a software company's own landing-page video or README is an "advertisement" under the NY, CA or PA laws, and only those three states plus Utah and Maine were examined. (d) The French, Italian, Dutch and Irish authority sites were not opened. The relation of the Official Journal entry C/2026/6005 (2026-11-12, one secondary page) to C(2026) 7702 is unknown, and so is what changed between the guidelines' first publication (2026-07-20) and the 2026-11-05 text.

## The ruleset

Severity is for an agent acting with no human in the loop. "Copy surface" means the set MKT-CLAIM defines: READMEs, pitch and landing pages, docs-site home pages, page titles and meta descriptions, manifest descriptions, release notes, email, and the string literals and JSX text that render on those pages. A surface is read from `doc_surface` (MKT-PAGE), never from its path. `<copy paths>` is the repository's list of those files. `$DQ` is the directory where the docs-quality-reference skill's checks are installed: `skills/docs-quality-reference/checks` in this catalog, and `.claude/rules/docs-quality/checks` in `ocx-sh/catalog` (`ls /home/mherwig/dev/ocx-sh/catalog/.claude/rules/docs-quality/checks`, 2026-11-27). Commands marked "block" are in [Check blocks](#check-blocks) below the table.

# MKT-VOICE-16: check_performer.py, run as `python3 -I check_performer.py <built page>`
import re, sys
from html.parser import HTMLParser
CAP = re.compile(r"synthetic performer|no human performer is depicted|modified or generated using artificial intelligence", re.I)
PERSON = re.compile(r"\b(person|people|presenter|host|actor|actress|avatar|spokesperson|speaker|narrator|voiceover|man|woman)\b", re.I)
MEDIA = {"img", "video", "audio"}
class P(HTMLParser):
    def __init__(s):
        super().__init__(); s.stack = []; s.figs = []; s.loose = []
    def handle_starttag(s, tag, a):
        a = dict(a)
        if tag == "figure":
            f = {"flag": "data-synthetic-performer" in a, "media": 0, "cap": "", "incap": False}
            s.figs.append(f); s.stack.append((tag, f))
        elif tag == "figcaption" and s.stack:
            s.stack[-1][1]["incap"] = True
        elif tag in MEDIA:
            f = next((x[1] for x in reversed(s.stack) if x[0] == "figure"), None)
            alt = a.get("alt", "") + " " + a.get("aria-label", "")
            if f is not None: f["media"] += 1
            if PERSON.search(alt) and not (f and f["flag"]): s.loose.append(alt.strip())
    def handle_endtag(s, tag):
        if tag == "figcaption" and s.stack: s.stack[-1][1]["incap"] = False
        if tag == "figure" and s.stack: s.stack.pop()
    def handle_data(s, d):
        if s.stack and s.stack[-1][1]["incap"]: s.stack[-1][1]["cap"] += d
p = P(); p.feed(open(sys.argv[1]).read()); bad = 0
for f in p.figs:
    if f["flag"] and not (f["media"] and CAP.search(f["cap"])):
        print("FAIL flagged figure has no media or no matching figcaption inside it"); bad = 1
for alt in p.loose: print("READ media alt names a person but its figure is not flagged:", alt)
print("exit", bad); sys.exit(bad)

## AI-agent failure modes

Ranked by how often each bites, grouped by mechanism.

1. **Default model style leaks into copy (every unconstrained draft).** The agent writes spaced em dashes, long rule-voiced sentences, "never" and "every", and no "you". An instruction against the dash helps some models fully and others only partly (GPT-4.1 still 3.86 per 1,000 under an explicit prohibition). Late-2026 models that drop the dash still write corrective framing and importance flags ("is more than a", "why X matters"). Measured: the fleet's 13.51 em dashes per 1,000 words, 16.9-word sentences and 0.42 contractions per 1,000. Caught by MKT-VOICE-03 (or DOC-PLAIN-01 where it runs), -09 and -20.
2. **Habitual authorship marking (observed in the fleet).** The agent adds "Generated with Claude" footers to pages (MKT-VOICE-14) or `Co-Authored-By: Claude` trailers to documentation commits, as in [ocx-sh/website@0477a6b](https://github.com/ocx-sh/website/commit/0477a6b3f26e40d7517cddde113864073714606e) (DOC-PLAIN-22).
3. **Fabricated authenticity.** The agent invents a motive, a production history or "we got tired of X". It writes a terminal transcript instead of recording one, renders a mockup as the product, generates a presenter for a launch video, or adds a "human-written" badge. Caught by MKT-VOICE-06, -17, -16 and -14.
4. **Checker misuse.** The agent runs `prose.py` on `.astro` or `.ts` and "fixes" TypeScript semicolons. It reads the page type from a path, or strips the pitch from a pitch page because docs-style says "no marketing open". It applies a register floor to a changelog. Caught by MKT-VOICE-11, -12 and -09.
5. **Treating tells as a verdict.** The agent cites a detector percentage, strips "ensure", "navigate" or emoji from human text, rewrites a non-native author's voice, or pushes text through a humanizer. Caught by MKT-VOICE-01, -02 and -04, and by DOC-PLAIN-09 and -10.
6. **Wrong disclosure model.** The agent treats Art. 50 as a blanket duty to label all AI text, or as no duty at all. It labels AI text to satisfy a US state law that exempts text. It treats 2026-12-02 as its own deadline, counts its own or a second model's review as human review, re-polishes text after sign-off, puts a performer label in the footer, embeds C2PA data and calls that the label, or signs with the test certificate. Caught by MKT-VOICE-14, -15, -16 and -18.
7. **Self-attestation.** The agent writes its own `Owner words:`, `Editorial review:` or `Editorial responsibility:` line, or commits `status = "reviewed"` under the owner's git identity. Caught by the forge and page checks in MKT-VOICE-06, -08 and -15, except in a solo-owner repository (Verdict 12).
8. **Word-list sprawl.** The agent starts a new banned list, swaps "leverage" for an equally empty "harness", or writes "do not use X" and primes the word. Caught by MKT-VOICE-10.
9. **Wrong shape in failure notices.** The agent writes a cheerful exclamation in an incident note, apologises three times, invents a reader step for an outage nobody can act on, or tells readers to "take appropriate action" in an advisory without naming the version or the credential. Caught by MKT-VOICE-13 and -19.

## What an agent must ask a human

- The voice file's principles, pass and fail sentences, register per surface, and its `reviewed` status (MKT-VOICE-08).
- The owner's own words for any motive, experience or origin sentence, posted where the forge records the owner as author (MKT-VOICE-06).
- Whether to rewrite existing text that a marker flags, above all text by a non-native author (MKT-VOICE-04).
- Any voluntary public statement about AI use, either "this README was written by a human" or a Slack-style "we disclose AI use" ([Slack](https://slack.design/articles/slack-content-design-in-the-age-of-ai/)).
- Who holds editorial responsibility, and whether to publish that contact on a public page, given the deliberate no-Impressum line at `privacy.html:47` (MKT-VOICE-15).
- Whether a borderline artefact counts as public interest: a security advisory, a compliance explainer, a sustainability line. No authority has ruled on software advisories. The default is in scope, and only the owner may record the opposite.
- Publishing AI imagery of a real person, customer or lookalike. That also needs consent, and endorsements belong to MKT-CLAIM-20. Generating any human performer, even a fictional one, for an ad, video or pitch page (MKT-VOICE-16).
- Whether the project's own landing-page video, pitch page or README is an "advertisement" under the New York, California or Pennsylvania laws. That is counsel's call.
- Buying a C2PA signing certificate and holding its key. Adopting the EU icon or a house label wording.
- Whether shipping a generative feature makes the project a provider under Art. 50(1) and (2), or a California "covered provider" now that SB 1000 dropped the 1,000,000-user threshold (per the committee analysis; the chaptered text was not read).
- Posting agent-drafted text to a venue that forbids it. The HN guidelines say "Don't post generated text or AI-edited text" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html), read 2026-11-27). The channels sub-program owns venue rules.

## Key sources

1. https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215: Commission guidelines on Art. 50, C(2026) 7702 (2026-11-05; first published 2026-07-20). Scope of public-interest text, the review exception, deepfake examples, the transition.
2. https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/129555: Code of Practice on transparency of AI-generated content (published 2026-06-10). Label placement and Commitment 4.
3. https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act: Commission Art. 50 FAQ (updated 2026-07-24). Deepfake test and fines.
4. https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-icons-labelling-ai-generated-content: EU AI icon page (updated 2026-11-25).
5. https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27006: Freeburg, em dash rates for 12 models and human essays (Table 1), and for 4 models under an explicit prohibition (Table 2) (2026-03-27, fetched 2026-11-27).
6. https://graphite.io/five-percent/research/ai-tells-opus-5-5-update: Graphite, per-model em dash rates and phrase-tell multipliers against pre-ChatGPT web articles (2026-10-01, vendor).
7. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26710: Park, Jeong and Kim, detector false positives under editing (2026-08-27, preprint).
8. https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ai-detectors-false-negatives: Epoch AI detector evaluation (2026-07-15). The strongest counter-evidence.
9. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11156: Sadasivan et al., detection limits and the paraphrase attack (v4 2025-01-17).
10. https://openai.com/index/new-ai-classifier-for-indicating-ai-written-text/: OpenAI classifier withdrawal (2023-07-20).
11. https://www.palegis.us/statutes/unconsolidated/law-information?sessYr=2026&sessInd=0&actNum=99: Pennsylvania Act 99 of 2026, synthetic content in advertising, text exempt (enacted 2026-10-22, in force 2026-12-21).
12. https://legislation.nysenate.gov/pdf/bills/2025/S8420A: New York S.8420-A, GBL 396-b synthetic-performer disclosure (in force 2026-06-09).
13. https://www.asa.org.uk/news/disclosure-of-ai-in-advertising-striking-the-balance-between-creativity-and-responsibility.html: ASA and CAP, no blanket UK AI-disclosure duty (2025-05-29).
14. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing: WikiProject AI Cleanup taxonomy (read 2026-11-27; advice, not policy). Em dash overuse filed as historical, "2022 to September 2026".
15. https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice/: Monzo channel matrix and apology rule (vendor style guide, undated).
