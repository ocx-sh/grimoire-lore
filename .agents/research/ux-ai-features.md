# UX-AI: what a human-facing LLM interface must disclose, show and refuse to render

## Verdict

1. **Disclosure is law, and the project's default is to disclose.** EU AI Act Article 50(1) and 50(5) have applied since 2 August 2026. The dive that read the Official Journal text confirmed this, and the omnibus did not move the date ([OJ 2024/1689](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32024R1689) Art. 113; [OJ 2026/1744](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32026R1744)). Every surface where a person converses with a model under the team's name states "AI" in visible, accessible text at or before the first turn. The "obvious" exception exists, but no agent may claim it. Only an owner may claim it, in a written audience assessment. The cost of disclosing is one line of text. The Commission reads the exception restrictively and names coding agents as in scope (guidelines C(2026) 7702, paras 30 and 42-45, [PDF](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215), 5 Nov 2026).
2. **The EU notice is the base, and two US states add conditions on top.** A clear AI statement at the first interaction meets EU Art. 50(1). In Maine it meets 10 MRS 1500-DD, which applies only where a chatbot used in trade and commerce could mislead a consumer into thinking they deal with a human ([statute](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/10/title10sec1500-DD-1.html)). Utah's general duty applies only when the individual clearly asks (13-75-103(1)). Its safe harbour (13-75-104) needs a clear and conspicuous disclosure "at the outset and throughout" ([SB 226](https://le.utah.gov/Session/2025/bills/enrolled/SB0226.pdf); conversation-mechanics.md:135). A first-turn notice alone does not reach that safe harbour. UX-AI-01 plus the persistent label UX-AI-03 does. Companion chatbots carry more. New York Part U (GBL art. 47, in force since 2025-11-05) requires a notice at the start (once per day suffices) and at least every three hours in a continuing interaction, plus a crisis-referral protocol ([S-3008C](https://legislation.nysenate.gov/pdf/bills/2025/S3008C), GBL 1701-1702). California SB 243 (Chapter 677 of 2025, chaptered text read) adds a not-human notice where a reasonable person would be misled, a published self-harm protocol, a three-hour break reminder for known minors, a "may not be suitable for some minors" statement and, from 2027-07-01, annual reporting (B&P 22602-22604; followup-q2.md). These duties are UX-AI-05, a conditional MUST, and the owner decides whether the product is in scope. Both states exclude customer-service, productivity, research and technical-assistance bots. From 2027-01-01, New York art. 48 (S9051-B, signed 2026-10-28 as Chapter 368) makes twelve "unsafe AI companion features" unlawful for a user unless the operator has determined that the user is not a minor. That is UX-AI-24, also a conditional MUST, and its scope is wider than art. 47.
3. **Machine-readable marking (Art. 50(2)) is a provider-layer duty, and agents never invent it.** For systems already on the market before 2 August 2026, the deadline is 2 December 2026 (Art. 111(4) as inserted by 2026/1744), five days after this artifact. An agent's job is to classify the outputs and raise the question as blocking. It never adds zero-width "watermarks" or stamps "AI-generated" headers on source code, which guidelines para 68 puts outside marking.
4. **Model output is untrusted input to the renderer, and images are the leak.** The measurement is the deciding fact. With default settings, `marked` 18.3.0, `markdown-it` 15.0.3 and `react-markdown` 10.1.0 all render a remote markdown image as a live fetch, and DOMPurify keeps it. Shipped products leaked conversation data this way: M365 Copilot EchoLeak ([CVE-2025-32711](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2025-32711), published 2025-06-11) and Salesforce Agentforce ForcedLeak ([Noma Security](https://noma.security/blog/forcedleak-agent-risks-exposed-in-salesforce-agentforce/), 2025-09-25, security-vendor research), among others listed by an independent aggregator ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/tags/markdown-exfiltration/), secondary). The project requires a renderer image hook, a CSP `img-src` with no remote wildcard, and a CI test that includes the reference-style image bypass.
5. **Trust cues must be checkable or absent.** Citations render only if they are members of the retrieved set, because a marker for a source that was never retrieved is fabricated by definition. Citation studies are background, not the basis ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848), 2023: 74.5% citation precision across four commercial engines). The project should not display an LLM's self-reported number as a probability unless the owner has measured its calibration for that model, prompt and task, and re-measures after every change (UX-AI-15, SHOULD). Five fetched studies (2023 to 2026, abstracts read) disagree. In one, verbalized confidence was better calibrated than token probabilities ([Tian et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975), EMNLP 2023). In two, it was overconfident ([Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063), ICLR 2024; [Sanz-Guerrero et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03437), ACL 2026 Findings). In one, the score moved with the prompt protocol ([Kim and Kang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27752), preprint, v4 2026-10-23). In one, Brier scores ran from 0.103 to 0.367 across frontier models ([ConfidenceBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20526), preprint, 2026-07-10). Calibration therefore belongs to one model and one protocol, and a SHOULD with a recorded measurement is the checkable form. A model-written rationale is not a trust device: explanations raised acceptance whether or not the answer was correct ([Bansal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14779)). Hedges come from a code condition rather than a blanket prompt line, which is a CONSIDER because the one study may not transfer ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00623)).
6. **Streamed text should not be announced per token.** A virtual screen reader logged 500 announcements for 500 tokens, 50 for per-sentence updates and 1 for on-completion. This is a simulation, not real assistive technology, and the tool's own README says it "should not replace" real screen reader testing (conversation-mechanics.md:185). So the rule is a SHOULD. The default is a non-live, navigable message body plus one completion announcement that says where the answer is. Sentence-level `role="log"` announcement is a user opt-in.
7. **The fleet has no human-facing LLM interface today** (measured, see Applied). The ruleset is dormant for product surfaces. Only 9 rules are MUST, and every MUST is LEGAL or MEASURED. Two things conflict with it now. The first is the grim MCP server, which hands publisher-written agent instructions to a host model with no data boundary (UX-AI-13, SHOULD). The second is the catalog's own DOC-PLAIN-08 gate, which flags an "AI-generated" label on a markdown page (measured below). UX-AI-01 and -07 now state that they win over it for marked disclosure labels.
8. **Documented gaps from wave 2 (findings, not open questions).** Each names what is missing.
   - Calibration (UX-AI-15): no fetched study measures verbalized confidence on open-ended chat, retrieval-augmented or coding answers, and none tests whether showing the number improves user decisions. Only abstract pages were read, not the PDFs (followup-q1.md). The rule stays SHOULD. Promote it only if a study shows miscalibration across current frontier chat models on such answers.
   - California SB 243 operative date (UX-AI-05): 2026-01-01 is CORROBORATED, not primary-verified. The chaptered text states no date. The bill author's office ([Sen. Padilla](https://sd18.senate.ca.gov/news/first-nation-ai-chatbot-safeguards-signed-law), 2025-10-13) and two secondary sources state it. A literal reading of Const. Art. IV s.8(c)(1) gives 2027-01-01 (`date -d "2025-10-13 + 90 days" +%F` printed `2026-01-11`). Either way the duties bind by 2027-01-01. Missing: the passage and enrollment dates from leginfo, which returns HTTP 403 to non-browser clients (`403 5610`), and the full text of Art. IV s.8(c)(2). The chaptered text was read in a Legislative Counsel PDF hosted by a law firm ([Sidley copy](https://www.sidley.com/en/-/media/resource-pages/ai-monitor/laws-and-regulations/cal-sb243-companion-chatbots.pdf?la=en)), not on a state host.
   - New York art. 48 (UX-AI-24): the definitions and the penalty were read in the as-passed S9051-B PDF, not in a chaptered copy of Chapter 368. The Attorney General's regulations under 1803 and the "permissible methods" of article 45, which 1801(1)(b) points to, were not read. Missing: the chaptered text and those regulations.
   - Scope of "AI companion" for docs and developer tools: the exclusions were read, but no court or Attorney General guidance was fetched. Scope stays an owner decision in writing.

## The ruleset

Severity: MUST, SHOULD or CONSIDER. "Basis" says whether a rule is LEGAL (primary text read), MEASURED, or DESIGN (guidance, vendor docs or studies). MUST is used only for LEGAL and MEASURED rows. Never describe a DESIGN rule to a user as required by law. Vendor sources are labelled "vendor".

Dropped as generic or low-yield: the "three to six suggestions" count (unmeasured), a report-a-bad-answer control (agents add it unprompted), the OpenAI Apps SDK card and carousel limits (host-specific), an explanation popover on the label (folded into UX-AI-17), label glow contrast (UX-A11Y contrast owns it), commit attribution (C10), a live region that exists empty at load (A11Y-ANN-2 owns it), and, in the revision, the Stop and Regenerate row (UX-AI-21) and the empty-state and suggestion row (UX-AI-23).

### Rendering model output

| ID | Rule | Rationale | Verification | Severity |

## Agent failure modes

The order is a judgement of frequency, based on what the default ("naive") build produces. Two of these were measured: renderer defaults, and 500 simulated announcements for a polite container that receives tokens.

**1. Training-data defaults ship as is (most frequent).**
- The agent uses `marked` or `markdown-it` with defaults, so live remote images appear, and with `marked` also raw script (UX-AI-09, -10, -12).
- It sets `aria-live="polite"` on the message container that receives tokens: 500 simulated announcements (UX-AI-19).
- It writes an AI SDK 4 `toolInvocations` renderer with `call` and `result` states only, so denial and error show as endless spinners (UX-AI-18).
- It renders `error.message` or `errorText` directly (UX-AI-20).
- It leaves Stop always visible, or Regenerate enabled during the stream (UX-AI-18).

**2. Misreading "safe" (frequent, and the costliest).**
- It treats "react-markdown is safe by default" as covering images. The claim covers XSS only, and the renderer still fetches the image plus a preload (UX-AI-09).
- It treats DOMPurify as an image policy (UX-AI-09).
- It writes a regex for `![](…)` and misses `![x][r]` (UX-AI-12).
- It allow-lists `docs.google.com`, a CDN with user uploads, a host with an open redirect, or a domain nobody renews (UX-AI-09).

**3. Disclosure treated as boilerplate.**
- The disclosure appears only in a footer, a tooltip or terms (UX-AI-01).
- The label is an icon-only sparkle, or an `aria-hidden` badge (UX-AI-02).
- The disclosure appears after the first reply, or as a one-time modal only (UX-AI-01, -03).
- A human-persona avatar or name appears with no disclosure (UX-AI-01).

**4. Legal confabulation.**
- Dates and fines are written from memory. For example, "Art. 50 applies in 2027", or no 2 Dec 2026 grace, or a grace applied to 50(1) (Decay).
- A design rule is labelled "required by the AI Act", or the copy claims compliance (UX-AI-08).
- The agent claims the "obvious" exception for a developer audience (UX-AI-01).
- It invents a zero-width watermark, or stamps "AI-generated" headers on generated code (UX-AI-06).
- It writes a zero-width check with `\\u200b` escapes, which PCRE never matches, so the check passes on a planted mark (UX-AI-06).
- It treats New York's three-hour notice as the only companion duty, and misses California's known-minor, publication and minors-suitability duties (UX-AI-05). Or it reads NY art. 48 as "protect users known to be minors" and leaves persona and memory features on by default (UX-AI-24, C13).

**5. Trust theatre.**
- "Confidence: 87%" taken from the model's own output (UX-AI-15).
- A calibration measured once and never re-run after a model or prompt change, so the number keeps showing under a new protocol (UX-AI-15).
- A "Why this answer" paragraph added to build trust (UX-AI-17).
- Citation numbers rendered as the model emitted them, or labelled "verified" (UX-AI-14).
- An "always say you might be wrong" line in the system prompt (UX-AI-16).

**6. Rule collisions (rare, but silent).**
- Obeying DOC-PLAIN-08 strips a required AI label from a markdown page (C11).
- An MCP server passes third-party instructions without a boundary, because the tool is "read-only" (UX-AI-13).

## What an agent must ask a human

- Whether the team is the **provider** or the **deployer** for each AI feature: own name or trademark, third-party model, white-label. This decides which of Art. 50(1), (2) and (4) apply.
- Whether the "obvious" exception applies. The default is to disclose, and the exception needs a written audience assessment and a legal sign-off.
- Whether the product is an **AI companion** (NY GBL 1700(4)) or a **companion chatbot** (CA B&P 22601(b)), reaches New York or California users, or serves known minors, and which of the UX-AI-05 duty flags apply (UX-AI-05).
- Whether a New York chat feature is a **covered AI companion** under art. 48, and which age-determination method is permissible under 1801(1)(b) (UX-AI-24).
- The machine-readable **marking technique** and whether to sign the Commission code of practice (UX-AI-06).
- Whether a feature publishes **public-interest text** or deep fakes, and who holds editorial responsibility (UX-AI-07).
- The **wording** of the disclosure, the not-human notice and the crisis-referral copy (legal, clinical and brand). An agent drafts options and never ships them alone.
- The **image and link host allow-list** (UX-AI-09).
- Which surfaces are **high-stakes** (UX-AI-17), and whether to show any confidence label at all (UX-AI-15 needs a user test the agent cannot run, and a number needs the owner's calibration record on labelled product data).
- **Memory** retention, scope and what reaches the model (UX-AI-22).
- Any statement that the product **complies with** a law (UX-AI-08), and any non-EU jurisdiction this ruleset does not cover.

## Open questions

### Another round

1. **Real-AT streaming.** How do NVDA (Firefox and Chrome), JAWS, VoiceOver (macOS and iOS) and TalkBack treat per-sentence appends to a `role="log"` region at streaming rate? Do they coalesce, drop or queue? This decides whether UX-AI-19 can return to MUST and whether its default should become per-sentence.
2. **Text marking in practice.** What does the final code of practice commit providers to for text (metadata, watermark, short-text exception)? Do the Anthropic, OpenAI and Google APIs mark text output so that an application on top of them meets Art. 50(2) without its own mark? This decides UX-AI-06's agent action.
3. **Other states.** Which US states other than New York, California, Utah and Maine enacted chatbot disclosure or companion-chatbot laws in 2026? Wave 2 did not search this. (The SB 243 text, the NY Part U date and NY art. 48 were answered in followup-q2.md; their remaining gaps are in Verdict 8.)
4. **Memory and GDPR.** Do GDPR access and erasure rights make UX-AI-22's view and delete controls law for EU users of an LLM memory feature?
5. **European Accessibility Act scope.** Does Directive (EU) 2019/882 bind a given AI chat surface, and from which date? Guidelines para 144 point to it, and the dive left it UNVERIFIED.
6. **Streaming markdown.** Does a half-streamed `![x](https://…` fetch before the image hook resolves in client-side React 19? Does `react-markdown` with `rehype-sanitize` change any row of the measurement?
