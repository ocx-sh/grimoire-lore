# Marketing topic map: practitioner writing

## Scope and method

Landscape scout. It finds the questions; it does not answer them. Pages were opened with WebFetch, not read from search snippets. A claim marked "unverified" came from a search result only, and no candidate depends on it.

Coverage gaps, stated plainly:

- The web search budget ran out mid-run. Not reached: Lenny Rachitsky benchmark posts (the archive page returned only one marketing post), Elena Verna (only the landing page loaded; the archive was rate-limited), Harry Dry articles (the site index loaded, the checklist URL returned 500), Julian Shapiro (julian.com returned 403 to WebFetch and to curl), Amanda Natividad's individual posts, Dave Gerhardt's own writing, Andy Raskin's original essay, Fletch's own site (DNS failure), Y Combinator library pages (404 or rate-limit), Corey Haines beyond the Swipe Files front page, Chris Walker's own posts, Peep Laja's own posts.
- For those authors, only claims marked "unverified" appear in the Contested section. Re-run this scout with the budget raised before treating them as covered.
- No LinkedIn or YouTube platform limit was fetched in this corpus. The platform-channel scouts own those; this map flags them as candidates only.

Verification commands for measurements: this corpus produced no local measurements. Every number is an external claim with an inline URL. The one local command run was `curl -sL -A "Mozilla/5.0" https://www.julian.com/guide/growth/landing-pages -o /tmp/j.html -w "%{http_code}\n"`, which printed `403` (Cloudflare challenge page, 60 characters of text).

## Summary of what the corpus says

1. The 2026 practitioner conversation is dominated by one shift: buyers research through AI assistants, search clicks fell, and measurement broke. Poyar, Indig, Fishkin and Ahrefs all write about it, with numbers dated 2026.
2. Positioning and homepage copy still carry the argument, but the testable claims are few. Most "rules" (headline benefit, one persona, 2-4 nav links) come from one handbook per author, with no controlled test cited.
3. Launch advice is old and stable (Paul Graham, 2013) but channel rules (Product Hunt, Show HN) are dated and must be re-fetched.
4. The three classic fights (gated vs ungated, brand vs performance, founder vs company) each have evidence on both sides, mostly vendor-authored. See Contested.
5. For a developer-tool project the checkable surface is narrow: whether pages render without JavaScript, whether pricing and docs are readable by machines, whether a launch post obeys the host's rules, whether claims are dated.
