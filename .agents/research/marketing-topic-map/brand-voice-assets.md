## Contested

- **AI-copy tells: use them or not.** Wikipedia's list gives concrete tells, yet the same page says detection tools are unreliable, human judgement is near chance, signs are not proof, and fixing surface features can hide real problems ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). Fetched abstracts agree: recursive paraphrasing significantly reduces detection rates ([arXiv 2303.11156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11156)) and detectors misclassify non-native English writing as AI ([arXiv 2304.02819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819)). Position for a rule: tells are smell flags for editing, never a gate.
- **How much personality in brand voice.** Mailchimp puts clarity over entertainment and allows dry humor ([source](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)); Monzo sets organic social to maximum wit and operational comms to none ([source](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice/)); Atlassian removes the wink for stressed users ([source](https://atlassian.design/foundations/content/voice-tone)); Slack's article describes its own voice as playful and witty while insisting on trust and candour ([source](https://slack.design/articles/slack-content-design-in-the-age-of-ai/)). They agree on direction (less personality when stakes rise) and differ on how much belongs on social channels.
- **Thumbnail metric.** TubeBuddy's creator-tool page recommends A/B testing thumbnails and measuring click-through rate to find the best performer ([source](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-click-through-rate/), undated, vendor selling the tool). YouTube's own test selects on highest watch time and says it optimises watch time over CTR ([source](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)), and its CTR help warns that clickbait can raise CTR while lowering view duration ([source](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154)).
- **Share-image ratio.** Facebook and LinkedIn document about 1.91:1 ([Facebook](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/sharing/webmasters/images/), [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a521928)); the only retrievable X source says landscape near 2:1 and is itself not official ([TSF](https://kb.theseoframework.com/kb/twitter-cards-and-x-sharing/)). Minimums also differ: Facebook 600x315 for large, LinkedIn 1200x627, X 300x157. One 1200x630 file satisfies all three; whether each platform crops the 0.01 difference is untested.
- **Banned words versus exceptions.** GOV.UK bans "deploy" and "key" in general but allows them in military or software and "unlocks something" contexts ([source](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/style-guides/a-to-z-style-guide/)); for a developer-tool audience "deploy" is correct, so a lifted list needs audience exceptions.
- **Favicon set size.** The 2021 six-file set ([source](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/how-to-favicon-in-2021-six-files-that-fit-most-needs)) versus MDN, which lists apple-touch sizes 120 to 180 and says a single 192x192 usually suffices ([source](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/link)).
- **Images of terminal output.** Google's style guide says never use images of terminal output, use text ([source](https://developers.google.com/style/images)); terminal GIFs and casts are the demo norm for CLIs ([VHS](https://github.com/charmbracelet/vhs)). Resolution depends on whether the content is demonstration of motion (media) or copyable commands (text); both must exist.
