Scope: the company or engineering blog, long-form technical posts, feeds, syndication, article metadata, AI-assistance disclosure. Documentation pages are covered by `docs-quality` and stay out. No local measurements were taken (this is a web survey), so no commands are listed; every external claim carries its URL.

## Contested

| Question | Side A | Side B |
|---|---|---|
| Canonical or noindex for syndicated copies | Canonical back to the original is the long-standing practice and is a strong signal per Google's canonical page (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls) | Ahrefs reports Google now recommends noindex on the copy, with canonical as fallback when partners refuse (https://ahrefs.com/blog/content-syndication/). The Google page fetched has no syndication section, so the noindex claim is secondhand |
| Is there a duplicate-content penalty | Ahrefs: Google confirmed there is none (https://ahrefs.com/blog/content-syndication/) | Google's spam policy treats duplicated or scraped content spread to manipulate rankings as abuse (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies); the risk is policy, not an automatic penalty |
| Long vs short posts | Ahrefs: longer posts rank for more long-tail terms (https://ahrefs.com/blog/blog-post-length/) | Same study: traffic and links fall beyond about 2,000 and 1,000 words, correlational; Google gives no length target in its helpful-content page |
| Full-text vs summary feed | RSS Board: full content is best supported (https://www.rssboard.org/rss-profile); Fly ships full (https://fly.io/blog/feed.xml) | Tailscale ships summaries (https://tailscale.com/blog/index.xml), which pulls readers to the site; no fetched source measures which wins |
| Do bylines and E-E-A-T help ranking | Google: trust matters, byline where expected (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) | Same page: E-E-A-T is not a specific ranking factor; Fly publishes without per-entry authors |
| Does freshness help | Ahrefs: AI-cited URLs fresher; date edits moved AI rank in one study (https://ahrefs.com/blog/content-decay/) | Google: do not change dates or add/remove content just to look fresh; will not help (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) |
| Is AI-assistance disclosure mandatory | EU Article 50 (applies 2026-08-02) requires it for public-interest text without editorial control (https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/) | Same article exempts text with human review and editorial responsibility, so a normal company blog may owe none; dev.to requires it anyway on its platform (https://dev.to/guidelines-for-ai-assisted-articles-on-dev) |
