# Codified law and platform policy that constrain software marketing

This is a landscape scout. It lists the rules somebody enforces, says who each binds and what it demands, and ranks the questions a marketing rule or skill must answer. It is not legal advice and the rules and skills built from it must not present themselves as such.

Method note. Pages were opened with WebFetch, which summarises with a small model, so exact figures were taken from the summary of the page and are marked "page" below. Three facts come only from search-result text (the page was not opened) and are marked "search only"; treat them as unverified. The shared web-search budget ran out mid-run, so UK PECR corporate-subscriber detail, German B2B case law, the status of the EU Digital Omnibus delay proposal, LinkedIn's paid-partnership policy and the DSA Article 26 text were not confirmed. They are listed under "Gaps" at the end. Several fetches failed with 403/404 or rate-limit errors (CRTC, ICO, EUR-Lex Directive 2006/114, FTC AI-claims blog); no content from those is used.

## What each regime demands

### United States

| Regime | Binds | Concrete obligation | Penalty or enforcement example | Mechanically checkable by an agent? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FTC Endorsement Guides (revised 2023) and Disclosures 101 | Advertisers, endorsers, agencies | Disclose any material connection (payment, free product, affiliate commission, employment, family) clearly and conspicuously in each post, at the start, in plain words ("Ad", "Sponsored by X"); a description-only or hashtag-pile disclosure is not enough; in video, disclose by audio and on screen; platform tools do not by themselves satisfy the duty ([page](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking), [page](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)) | Section 5 FTC Act enforcement; advertiser liable for agency and endorser conduct | Partly. A lint can flag a post draft that mentions a partner, affiliate link or free product without "ad" or "sponsored" in the first line. Whether a connection exists is a fact the agent must ask for. |
| FTC rule on consumer reviews and testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465, effective 2024-10-21 | Any business selling or buying reviews, plus insiders | Bans fake or AI-generated reviews and testimonials, buying sentiment-conditioned reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, review suppression by threat or false accusation, and buying or selling fake social indicators such as bot followers ([FTC Q&A, Nov 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers), [press release 2024-08-14](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). The FTC pages give no dollar figure. | Civil penalties for knowing violations; a search result (not opened) quotes up to $51,744 per violation, inflation-adjusted, so verify before citing (search only: [Federal Register notice 2024-08-22](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials)) | Partly. A check can flag testimonial text with no named, real source, and any "buy followers/stars" step in a launch plan. Authenticity of a testimonial is not machine-checkable. |
| FTC Operation AI Comply, 2024-09-25 | Sellers of AI products and "AI income" schemes | Claims about what an AI product does need evidence; DoNotPay paid $193,000 for "robot lawyer" claims; Rytr was barred from review-generation services; three income schemes were halted by courts ([press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes)). Workado's "98 percent accurate" AI detector tested at 53 percent, proposed consent order 2025-04-28 ([press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/ftc-order-requires-workado-back-artificial-intelligence-detection-claims)) | Settlements, receiverships, bans on unsupported effectiveness claims, 3-year-plus compliance reporting | Partly. A check can flag unqualified numeric accuracy or "replaces X" claims lacking a linked methodology or benchmark file. |
| FTC advertising substantiation policy (1984) | Every advertiser | Hold a reasonable basis before the claim runs; if the ad says "tests show", have tests; implied claims count ([policy statement](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation)) | Section 5 unfairness and deception | Partly. "Nx faster" or "fastest" needs a linked reproducible benchmark with command, hardware and date; presence of the link is checkable, its validity is not. |
| Negative-option, "free" and click-to-cancel | Subscription sellers | The FTC's Click-to-Cancel rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 2025-07-08 on procedural grounds (search only: [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20250801-eighth-circuit-vacates-the-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-but-federal-and-state-regulators-likely-to-remain-active)). ROSCA and state auto-renewal laws remain; the EU Digital Fairness Act proposal of 2026-11-11 targets cancellation, auto-renewal and trial-to-paid conversion ([Commission page](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/digital-fairness-act_en)). I could not open the ROSCA text. | State laws "as strict or stricter" per the same search result | Partly. A check can compare a pricing page's trial terms against the cancel flow path; legal sufficiency is not checkable. |
| CAN-SPAM | Any sender of commercial email, B2B included | Accurate From/Reply-To and subject, label as promotional, valid postal address, working opt-out for 30 days after send, honour opt-out within 10 business days, no fee for opt-out, responsible for vendors ([FTC guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)) | Up to $53,088 per violating email (page) | Yes for templates: postal address, unsubscribe link, header presence, promotional label. |

| GDPR and ePrivacy | Any controller or sender targeting EU residents | GDPR recital 47 says direct marketing "may be regarded as carried out for a legitimate interest" subject to a balancing test and reasonable expectations ([recital 47](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-47/)). ePrivacy Article 5(3) governs storing or reading information on a device (cookies, pixels); EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 version 2.0 define its technical scope (the guideline's detail was unreadable in the fetch; [consultation page](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/documents/public-consultations/2023/guidelines-22023-technical-scope-art-53-eprivacy_en)). | GDPR fines (amount not verified here) | Partly. | German UWG section 7 | Senders of electronic advertising in Germany | Email advertising without the recipient's prior express consent is always deemed harassment; telephone advertising to non-consumers needs at least presumed consent; existing-customer exception requires four cumulative conditions (address obtained at a sale, own similar products, no objection, clear objection notice at collection and each use) ([gesetze-im-internet.de section 7](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html)).
