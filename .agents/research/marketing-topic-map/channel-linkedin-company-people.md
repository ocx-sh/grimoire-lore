# LinkedIn, organisation and people: landscape scout

## Method and limits of this survey

Every platform fact below was fetched on 2026-11-27 from LinkedIn Help, the LinkedIn User Agreement, the FTC, the EU Commission, the UK ASA or Buffer. No fact comes from memory. Two limits shaped the result and the reader must weigh them.

1. The shared web-search budget was spent before this worker started, so discovery was by direct fetch of known URLs and LinkedIn Help topic indexes. Several named sources could not be opened: the Edelman-LinkedIn thought-leadership report (HTTP 403), the LinkedIn B2B Institute pages (HTTP 999 or 400), the Refine Labs studies (domain check rate-limited; the homepage fetched says nothing about reach), Dave Gerhardt, and any critique of Justin Welsh (his homepage fetched; Wikipedia 404). Those are listed as GAPS in the candidate table, not summarised.
2. LinkedIn Help pages show only relative dates ("Last updated: 9 months ago"), never an absolute date. Every "date" below for a Help page is therefore the relative age as shown on 2026-11-27. Where a source states an absolute date it is given.

A finding worth its own line: the Help pages are inconsistent with each other and some are three or more years old. A rule that pins a number to "LinkedIn Help" must pin the article URL and carry a refresh cadence.

## Landscape in six clusters

1. Surfaces and their hard limits (Pages, profiles, posts, documents, polls, newsletters, events). Mostly checkable by a limit table.
2. Reach mechanics and measurement (feed distribution, analytics windows, noise added to demographics). Mostly judgement; the one machine-checkable part is the retention and definition facts.
3. Page versus person (who posts, who is tagged, how employees attach to a Page, collaboration posts). Decision-heavy.
4. Employee advocacy and disclosure (FTC 16 CFR 255, FTC 2024 rule on insider reviews, ASA, EU). Legal, checkable by a disclosure pattern.
5. Outreach and automation (invitation limits, note length, User Agreement section 8.2, engagement pods). Checkable.
6. Technical credibility (profile and post of an engineer). The evidence is thinnest here; the corpus fetched offers no studied examples (see GAPS).
