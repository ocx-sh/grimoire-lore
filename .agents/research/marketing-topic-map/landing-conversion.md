# Landing page and product copy — landscape map

## Scope and method

Corpus: the product page and its copy, README-as-landing-page, and service pages. Every URL below was opened with a fetch tool on 2026-11-27. Repair pass, same day: Fletch (home, sprint pricing, FAQ, and four newsletter articles), Copyhackers (three articles) and the FTC endorsement-guide page were fetched and now support the rows that previously had no source. Julian Shapiro's guide returned HTTP 403 on two URLs, so it is NOT represented in this map and no Shapiro claim is made. Joanna Wiebe's Substack exposes no post text on its landing page, and Copyhackers' site returned nothing on the rule of one, so that rule is attributed only to Stripe Atlas. Still unfetched: Wynter's report PDFs, CXL, Navattic (demo benchmarks), the European Accessibility Act text. Web search was exhausted, so sources were reached by direct URL. Accessibility, Core Web Vitals, A/B statistics and consent law were removed from the table as outside this axis (page content and copy); they belong to the measurement, accessibility and legal scouts.

Boundary with existing sets: `docs-quality` owns page types, plain English and navigation of documentation. `css-theming` owns tokens and dark mode. This map covers the marketing page: what it must contain, how its copy is written, and the claims it makes lawfully. Documentation pages are out of scope; README-as-landing-page is in scope for the first screen only.

## Measurement taken on the fleet

Command (run from `/home/mherwig/dev`):

```
for f in ocx-sh/*/README.md grimoire-rs/*/README.md; do w=$(wc -w <"$f"); i=$(head -40 "$f" | grep -ciE 'install|curl|brew|cargo'); h=$(grep -c '^#' "$f"); echo "$f words=$w install_in_first40=$i headings=$h"; done
```

Result: 17 READMEs, word counts from 110 (`ocx-sh/website/README.md`) to 2385 (`grimoire-rs/vscode/README.md`). Four READMEs show no install hint in the first 40 lines (`e2e-index`, `index`, `rules_ocx`, `website`); some are libraries or infrastructure repos where that may be correct, which is why the check needs a repository-kind condition. `ocx-sh/ocx/README.md` (254 words, 13 headings, 7 install hits in the first 40 lines) is the tightest first screen. The count is a crude lexical proxy, not a judgement of quality.

## Findings that shape the candidates

1. Readers scan. A 1997 study found 79% of users always scanned a new page and 16% read word by word; the concise-plus-scannable-plus-objective version scored 124% better usability ([NN/g, 1997-09-30](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/)). The study is 29 years old; its direction is repeated in newer eye-tracking, but the percentages are not current.
2. Attention is front-loaded but less than it was. Above-the-fold share of viewing time fell from about 80% in 2010 to about 57% in 2018, and the top two screens still hold 74% ([NN/g, 2018-04-15](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)).
3. The conversion benchmark is vendor data and low for software. Unbounce's median is 6.6% across industries and 3.8% for SaaS, from 41,000 pages, 464 million visitors, Q4 2024, page updated 2025-03-04 ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/average-conversion-rates-landing-pages/), [Unbounce SaaS](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)).
4. Reading level is a measurable lever with a correlation caveat. Unbounce reports pages at 5th to 7th grade level converting at 12.9% against 2.1% at professional level, and 250 to 725 words as the best-converting length ([Unbounce SaaS](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)). The data is observational and pages differ by audience and offer; treat it as a prior, not a rule.
5. Most software sites fail the same checks. Baymard's 2025 SaaS benchmark: about 20% of sites do not show the product interface well enough to preview, 33% lack industry pages, 44% score mediocre or worse on plan matrices, 93% do not link matrix features to feature pages, 25% hide features behind "View More" ([Baymard, 2025-03-18](https://baymard.com/blog/saas-website-ux-best-practices)).
6. Low-traffic sites cannot detect small effects. A worked example needs 59,182 visitors per variation for a 10% relative lift at 5% baseline, 90% power, 99% confidence ([Convert, updated 2026-09-01](https://www.convert.com/blog/a-b-testing/statistical-power/)). Convert's own small-site guidance recommends about 10K visitors per variant and about 1,000 conversions, and says tests on a few hundred visitors a day are impractical ([Convert, updated 2025-02-26](https://www.convert.com/blog/a-b-testing/i-dont-have-enough-traffic-to-a-b-test-now-what/)).
7. Buyers research before they talk. In a Wynter survey of 100 B2B SaaS CMOs at companies above $50M revenue, 100% visited the vendor website before buying and 46% wanted to try the product before talking to sales; 9% began with a Google search, 24% used AI tools to compare vendors ([Wynter, 2025-06-16](https://wynter.com/post/why-your-website-is-the-dealbreaker)). The sample is marketing leaders at large firms, not developers; it does not transfer to a developer-tool audience without a check.
8. Pricing is the most wanted B2B information. NN/g ranked price highest of 28 information types, 29% ahead of availability, and B2B sites hid information behind forms and omitted prices ([NN/g, 2006-05-31](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-usability/)). Old, but the direction is still the live debate (see Contested).
9. Studied exemplars use different shapes. Linear leads with a product mockup, two CTAs ("Get started", "Contact sales"), no pricing in the fetched hero content, two named testimonials and a count ("over 50,000 product teams"), then a changelog before the closing CTA ([linear.app, fetched 2026-11-27](https://linear.app)). Stripe leads with a headline, subhead, two sign-up CTAs, logo carousels, segment blocks and a developer section, and surfaces pricing as a short closing block plus a nav link ([stripe.com, fetched 2026-11-27](https://stripe.com)). Both pages change; the observation is dated, not a rule.
10. Fletch treats the H1 as "the most important 5-10 words of your entire business" whose job is to tell the visitor "I'm in the right place", built from a reference point (product category or job-to-be-done) plus differentiation; failure modes are "too broad" ("automate systems") and "too lofty" ("drive revenue"); "spark curiosity" is rejected ([Fletch, 2026-04-07](https://fletchpmm.com/newsletter/how-to-write-an-h1)). Fletch says the guidance targets B2B software, not consumer products.
11. Fletch's three positioning strategies are: enter a mature category, enter an immature category, create a new category; the immature-category test is to ask an AI assistant for the top vendors ([Fletch, 2026-06-05](https://fletchpmm.com/newsletter/there-are-only-three-positioning-strategies)). The article does not state homepage copy changes; the copy mapping in candidate 9 is an inference.
12. Fletch's sprint page is itself a service page: three price tiers by revenue ($10,000 / $20,000 / $30,000), a day-by-day two-week process, named deliverables, five client testimonials with numbers, and a stated exclusion list ([FletchPMM pricing](https://fletchpmm.com/pricing)). Thoughtbot's page shows the opposite shape: no prices, no process, a logo wall of about 27, three case studies and about five "Let's talk" links ([thoughtbot.com, fetched 2026-11-27](https://thoughtbot.com)).

| Should the CTA sit in the hero? | Copyhackers: a button placed too early, such as in a hero section, can attract unqualified visitors; hold it until the pitch ([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/)). | Linear, Stripe and Thoughtbot all place CTAs in the hero ([Linear](https://linear.app), [Stripe](https://stripe.com), [thoughtbot.com](https://thoughtbot.com)); Fletch's page repeats "Work with FletchPMM" ([FletchPMM](https://fletchpmm.com/pricing)). | No conversion data in either position. | Does a long page or a short page convert better? | Unbounce: 250 to 725 words is best on its data ([Unbounce SaaS](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)). | Wynter's example grew about three times in length after adding customer language and clicks rose (vendor case, [Wynter](https://wynter.com/post/how-to-use-voice-of-customer-data-to-write-copy-for-technical-buyers)); Stripe Atlas says short in mature markets, educational in emerging ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/au/atlas/guides/landing-page-copy)). | Length follows awareness and maturity; Fletch's strategy split (mature versus immature category) is the same axis ([Fletch](https://fletchpmm.com/newsletter/there-are-only-three-positioning-strategies)). |
