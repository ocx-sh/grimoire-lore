# Marketing canon: landscape scout

## Method and limits

Scope: 14 named works or schools from the corpus list: Dunford, Moore, Ries and Trout, Ogilvy, Schwartz, Hopkins, Cialdini, StoryBrand, jobs to be done, Sharp and Ehrenberg-Bass, Binet and Field, Mom Test, Lean Analytics, Traction. Pages were opened with WebFetch. No shell measurement was made, so there are no measurement commands. This is a repair pass: the first run opened 19 pages; this pass opened 22 more (41 in total) and rewrote every row that had no source or was not a question.

What the repair did and did not achieve:

- Now read from a fetched page: Schwartz awareness and sophistication stages (reader's notes of the book, an archive.org item, not the book itself), Hopkins (the 1923 text itself, public domain), Mom Test rules (a 2026 secondary guide that cites Fitzpatrick; the book site lists topics only), the four forces of progress (jobstobedone.org), Lean Analytics stages (the authors' site), Cialdini on misuse (the Cialdini Institute page), the origin of the 95:5 figure (Dawes's own post), Commoncog's dated notes on Dunford.
- Still not verified from any fetched page: the nineteen Traction channels and the Bullseye steps (tractionbook.com refuses connections; Penguin, Goodreads, ProductPlan, GrowthMentor and Wikipedia returned 404 or nothing), the StoryBrand seven parts (storybrand.com shows only a three-step process, a five-part Messaging Canvas and four rule headlines; Wikipedia names the "7 Part Framework" and the year 2017 but not the parts), the 22 laws of Ries and Trout, Ogilvy's headline and long-copy rules, Moore's seven positioning slots from a primary source (a secondary summary is used), a direct statement of Sharp's position on targeting, and the Binet and Field report figures from IPA or LinkedIn (the IPA report page gives no figures; Wikipedia is used). No candidate row below depends on an unverified list. Where a framework is only named, the row asks a question that stands on a verified source and says so.
- Web search was exhausted early in the first run and was unavailable in this one; every page here was reached by direct URL.
- Most canon is books. Where a page is a summary or a reader's notes, the source column says so, and a deep-dive should read the book.

Axis correction: the first run included legal and compliance topics (the FTC rule on reviews, FTC social disclosure guidance, EU AI Act Article 50, accessibility of the disclosure, and a metric-validity row built on the FTC rule). Those are not positioning, messaging or persuasion frameworks. They are removed from this file and belong to a legal and platform-policy scout. Sharp, Binet and Field, and Lean Analytics are named in this corpus's brief, so they stay.

## Frameworks and what each forces a writer to decide before writing a word

| Framework | Decisions required first | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Dunford, Obviously Awesome | In this order: competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value and proof of that value, target market characteristics, market category, then (optional, "potentially dangerous") relevant trends. Pick one of three styles: Head to Head, Big Fish Small Pond, Create a New Game. Review positioning every 6 months and after major market events. Ask customers what they would use if the product did not exist, not about their problems. | [Commoncog notes](https://commoncog.com/obviously-awesome/), originally published 2019-05-28, updated 2025-01-07; [aprildunford.com](https://www.aprildunford.com/) says positioning "is the starting point for a company's messaging" |
| Moore, Crossing the Chasm | One beachhead segment; a whole product for it; a positioning statement with seven slots: target customer, need, product, category, key benefit, primary alternative, primary differentiation. | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_the_Chasm) (chasm named, whole product not defined); slots from [HELIX summary](https://documentdrivendx.github.io/helix/research/geoffrey-moore-positioning/), a paraphrase |
| Ries and Trout, Positioning | Which slot in the buyer's mind the product takes. Book 1981, articles 1969 and 1972. The 22 laws were not retrievable. | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positioning_(marketing)); [ries.com](https://www.ries.com/) lists no laws |
| Ogilvy | Research first; advertising's job is selling. Headline and long-copy rules not retrievable. | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ogilvy_(businessman)) |
| Hopkins, Scientific Advertising (1923) | Which single variant is tested and how the response is keyed back to it. Headline picks out the people you can interest. Claims must be specific. | [Hopkins full text on archive.org](https://dn721803.ca.archive.org/0/items/scientific-advertising/Scientific%20Advertising_djvu.txt), chapters 1, 5, 7, 13, 15 |
| Schwartz, Breakthrough Advertising | Reader's awareness (most aware, product aware, solution aware, problem aware, completely unaware) decides where the headline starts. Market sophistication (stage 1 first in market through stage 5, a market that no longer believes claims) decides whether to state the claim, enlarge it, add a new mechanism, elaborate the mechanism, or switch to identification with the prospect. | [Reader's notes of the book](https://dn721308.ca.archive.org/0/items/breakthrough-advertising-notes-1/BreakthroughAdvertisingNotes%20(1)_djvu.txt), not the book; stage 3 and 4 wording is the notes' paraphrase |
| Cialdini, Influence | Which of seven principles (reciprocity, liking, social proof, authority, scarcity, commitment and consistency, unity) a claim invokes, and whether the underlying fact is true. | [cialdini.com](https://www.cialdini.com/): influence gives "true reasons"; manipulation uses "fake deadlines, false popularity or borrowed authority" |
| StoryBrand | Customer as hero, brand as guide. The seven parts were not retrievable. | [storybrand.com](https://www.storybrand.com/), [canvas](https://www.storybrand.com/messaging-canvas/), [four rules](https://www.storybrand.com/the-4-rules-of-messaging/), [Wikipedia on Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Miller_(author)) (7 Part Framework, 2017 book) |
| JTBD | Which school: a job as progress with forces and a story (Christensen, Moesta), or a job as a task with measurable desired outcomes (Ulwick). The four forces: push, pull, anxiety, habit. | [Christensen Institute](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/), [jobstobedone.org](https://jobstobedone.org/), [Strategyn](https://www.strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/) |
| Sharp / Ehrenberg-Bass | Reach all category buyers, including light buyers, rather than narrow targeting; penetration over loyalty. | [Ehrenberg via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_loyalty): buyers hold portfolios of brands; penetration "does not guarantee that they will stay loyal". | Binet and Field | Brand versus activation budget share and horizon. | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Binet); [IPA B2B page](https://ipa.co.uk/effworks/marketing-marketing-v2/five-principles-of-growth-in-b2b-marketing/) |

## Candidate topics

Priority is for developer-tool open source marketed through READMEs, docs sites, release notes, LinkedIn and YouTube. The last column says what an agent-run rule or skill could check. "Covered" is judged against css-theming, docs-quality, code-docs and the installer sets.

## Contested

| Question | Side A | Side B |
|---|---|---|
| Is a fixed brand to activation split valid? | Binet and Field: 60:40 consumer, about 46:54 B2B ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Binet)) | Sharp, 2022: "very misleading", IPA award entries are an unsuitable dataset (same page). |
| Is 95:5 a rule? | Dreamdata: "the simple yet transformative idea that 95% of your potential buyers aren't in the market" ([summary](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/the-955-rule-why-b2b-growth-starts-long-before-the-purchase)) | Dawes, its originator: "not meant to be a precise rule", a heuristic from a five-year switching cycle ([post, 2021](https://johndawes.info/the-955-rule/)). |
| Narrow beachhead versus broad reach | Moore: dominate one niche ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_the_Chasm)); Dunford: Big Fish Small Pond ([Commoncog](https://commoncog.com/obviously-awesome/)) | Ehrenberg: buyers hold portfolios of brands, penetration does not equal loyalty ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_loyalty)). Sharp's own statement on targeting was not retrievable. |
| Does the chasm exist? | Moore | Rogers: "no support for this claim of a chasm" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_the_Chasm)) |
| Who invented positioning? | Ries and Trout | Fox: they "resurrected" it; Ogilvy mid-1950s ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positioning_(marketing))) |
| What to ask customers | Mom Test: ask about past behavior; show me how you solve it today ([Stepanenko guide](https://dn710903.ca.archive.org/0/items/stepanenko-guide-27-customer-discovery-en/27-customer-discovery_djvu.txt)) | Dunford: "Don't ask customers about their problems"; ask what they would use if the product did not exist ([Commoncog](https://commoncog.com/obviously-awesome/)). |
| What is a job? | Christensen Institute: progress in a circumstance, with functional, social and emotional forces ([page](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/)); four forces push, pull, anxiety, habit ([jobstobedone.org](https://jobstobedone.org/)) | Ulwick: "the task, goal, or objective a customer is trying to accomplish", judged by measurable outcomes, quantitative where design thinking is qualitative ([Strategyn](https://www.strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/)). |
| Be specific, or switch to identification? | Hopkins: "No generality has any weight whatever" ([ch. 7](https://dn721803.ca.archive.org/0/items/scientific-advertising/Scientific%20Advertising_djvu.txt)) | Schwartz stage 5: when the market no longer believes claims, "the emphasis switches from the promise and mechanism, to identification with the prospect himself" ([notes](https://dn721308.ca.archive.org/0/items/breakthrough-advertising-notes-1/BreakthroughAdvertisingNotes%20(1)_djvu.txt)). |
