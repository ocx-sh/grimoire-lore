## What differs from product marketing

- The buyer is a buying group. Forrester reports 13 people on average in a buying decision ([Forrester, 2024-12-04](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/state-of-business-buying-2024/)). A services page must serve a champion, an economic buyer and a risk checker.
- The product is a promise of judgement by named people. Proof (client names, outcomes, certifications) is the whole asset, and it is the thing an unsupervised agent is most tempted to invent.
- Sales cycles are long, so most readers are out of market on any day (the 95:5 frame, which is contested below).
- Review platforms, email law and security collateral carry enforceable rules that product marketing for a free open-source CLI rarely meets.

## Contested

- **95:5 as a planning rule.** For: the Ehrenberg-Bass B2B report treats most B2B buyers as out of market and frames advertising as memory-building ([Marketing Science, Nov 2023](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/how-b2b-brands-grow)); a practitioner blog repeats it and says the split varies by category ([The Strategy Story](https://thestrategystory.com/blog/the-95-5-rule-on-linkedin-why-most-b2b-ad-budgets-chase-the-wrong-buyers/)). Against or qualifying: the same blog admits the split varies, and neither page shows data. A claim that LinkedIn's CMO moved this to 99:1 came from a search snippet in pass one and is NOT verified. Agent consequence: never state "95% of buyers are not in market" as fact.
- **Do the FTC fake-review rule and endorsement guides cover B2B?** The FTC Q&A says the rule's testimonial definitions "do not include endorsements by business or other entities", and only section 465.6 reaches entities, while entities such as agencies can still be liable ([FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). Against a narrow reading: the FTC endorsement guidance treats a paid or employee connection as disclosable regardless of audience ([FTC endorsement Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)), and G2 and Clutch apply their own rules to B2B ([G2](https://legal.g2.com/community-guidelines), [Clutch](https://clutch.co/methodology)). The 16 CFR 465 text itself was unreadable. Conservative agent rule: never fabricate proof for any audience.
- **Is Clutch paid placement distorting?** Clutch says sponsorship affects default placement but not the score ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/methodology)); the same page shows buyers see sponsored order by default. Both statements are Clutch's own; no independent critique was fetched.
- **Public price versus value-based "contact us" pricing.** For pricing on value, not time: Stark ([jonathanstark.com](https://jonathanstark.com/)) says billing for time is a mistake and that getting faster should raise profit. Qualifying: the value-based pricing literature says quantifying value in B2B exchanges is hard, data-hungry and does not guarantee cost recovery ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value-based_pricing)). Neither addresses whether to publish a price on a page; Weiss's page gives no reasoning ([alanweiss.com](https://www.alanweiss.com/)). Unresolved.
- **Narrow versus broad positioning.** No credible source on the opposing side was read, and Enns, Baker and Morgan themselves were unreadable, so this item has no two-sided citation. It must be fetched by a dive before any rule states it.
