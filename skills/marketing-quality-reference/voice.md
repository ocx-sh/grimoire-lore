# Voice and AI authorship

Owns the voice file, the house style for lines an agent adds to a copy surface,
the ban on detectors, humanizers and claims about who wrote a page, the review and
label duties for public-interest text and generated media, and the shape of
failure notices. Evidence-free claims are `MKT-CLAIM-02`, `06`, `08` and `09`.
Which page is a pitch is `MKT-PAGE`. Product word bans are `MKT-POS-09` and `13`. A "copy surface" is the set
`MKT-CLAIM` defines (READMEs, pitch and landing pages, docs-site home pages, page
titles and meta descriptions, manifest descriptions, release notes, email, and the
string literals and JSX text rendered on those pages), read from `doc_surface`
(`MKT-PAGE`), never from its path.

Set `CHECKS=.claude/skills/marketing-quality-reference/checks` and
`DQ=.claude/skills/docs-quality-reference/checks` (under `$CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR/skills/`
when installed globally), and `COPY` to the copy surface files as an array, for
example `COPY=(README.md CHANGELOG.md docs/index.md)`. Run from the repository root.

## Boundary With docs-quality

- **Em dashes.** Where a repository runs `DOC-PLAIN-01`, it wins on every file its checker reads, docs-site home pages included. MKT-VOICE-03 and 04 apply only to the rest. `UX-TEXT-23` covers CLI output.
- **Authorship.** `DOC-PLAIN-08` bans badges and chatbot leftovers on `.md` pages, `DOC-PLAIN-09` bans wording a finding as a claim about who wrote a page, and `DOC-PLAIN-22` governs `Co-Authored-By` trailers (this file sets no trailer rule). MKT-VOICE-14 extends the badge ban to `.astro` and `.ts` copy, email and release bodies.
- **Word lists and tools.** Generic tells are `DOC-PLAIN-10` and `12`, claim words `MKT-CLAIM-02` and `09`: build no third list (MKT-VOICE-10). `strip_prose.py` and `prose_marks.awk` in `$DQ` read `.md` and `.mdx` only (MKT-VOICE-11). `DOC-EX-12` is the base rule for recordings; MKT-VOICE-17 applies it to pitch pages and README first screens.

## The Voice File

`.agents/voice.toml` (**pinned** location) holds one register per surface and one
pass and one fail sentence per principle: principles alone are uncheckable. The
owner decides voice. The agent drafts a missing file, writes `ASK-OWNER` where no
repository text supports a field, and never sets `status = "reviewed"`.

```toml
status = "unreviewed"    # reviewed | unreviewed; only the owner writes "reviewed"
review_by = 2027-05-27
owner = "ASK-OWNER"
[[principle]]
name = "plain"
pass = "Run `mytool init`, then edit the file it prints."
fail = "Leverage our best-in-class initialisation workflow."
[surface.pitch]          # one row per surface: pitch home readme release-note email notice
person = "second"        # second | first-plural | impersonal
contractions = "yes"     # yes | no
humour = "low"           # none | low | medium | high
```

## Rules

No detector and no style marker ever gates. MKT-VOICE-03, 04, 09, 10, 11, 12 and 20
bind lines the agent adds: a hit in existing text is a report, never a rewrite
trigger. Ordinary marketing text needs no AI disclosure (Art. 50(4) excludes
advertising text unless it makes health, safety or sustainability claims); media is
where the legal duty bites. Not legal advice. MKT-VOICE-05 is retired and its number is not reused: its criterion survives in [Not Studied](#not-studied).

## Limits

- **Agent and owner are indistinguishable in git** (2 AI trailer lines in 177
  measured README commits). MKT-VOICE-08 and 15 check a pull-request approval
  event instead, where GitHub refuses self-approval. A solo-owner repository cannot
  produce one: there the owner flips `reviewed` and publishes in-scope text by
  hand. MKT-VOICE-06 and `MKT-POS-04` fail the same way against an agent that
  holds the owner's token.
- **Self-attestation.** An agent that writes its own `Owner words:` or `Editorial review:` line is caught only by the forge checks.

## Ask the Owner

- The voice file's principles, sentences, registers and `reviewed` status (MKT-VOICE-08), and the owner's own words for any motive, experience or origin sentence, posted where the forge records the owner as author (MKT-VOICE-06).
- Whether to rewrite existing text a marker flags, above all by a non-native author (MKT-VOICE-04), and any voluntary public statement about AI use.
- Who holds editorial responsibility and whether to publish that contact. A site that publishes none cannot publish in-scope text (MKT-VOICE-15). Whether a borderline artefact (an advisory, a compliance explainer) is public interest: the default is in scope.
- Publishing AI imagery of a real person or lookalike (it needs consent; endorsements belong to MKT-CLAIM-20), generating any human performer (MKT-VOICE-16), and buying a C2PA signing certificate.
- Whether the project's own video, pitch page or README is an "advertisement" under the NY, CA or PA laws, and whether a generative feature makes it an Art. 50(1) and (2) provider: counsel's call.

## Not Studied

- Whether copy that follows MKT-VOICE-06, 07 and 20 is rejected less often, and whether MKT-VOICE-13 and 19 notices read better: all reader evidence is accusatory comments or vendor practice. No pre-LLM baseline exists for marketing email or release announcements, and the notice corpus is skewed to two vendors.
- The chaptered texts of California SB 1000 and SB 1050 returned HTTP 403. Only NY, CA, PA, UT and ME were examined, the French, Italian, Dutch and Irish authority sites were not opened, and what changed between the guidelines' 2026-07-20 and 2026-11-05 texts is unknown.
- **Catalog maintenance, not an agent rule.** Admit a marker to an AI-tell warning list only with a measured pre-LLM baseline: a pooled rate of at most 0.30 per 1,000 words and at most 1 triggering document among 26 or more. Never list emoji, bold-lead bullets, one-word triads, `navigat*` or `ensur*`: they occur in 2022 human READMEs at 3.47, 0.45, 1.34, 0.23 and 0.33 per 1,000 words. The excess-word set moves by model era ([Kobak et al., arXiv 2406.07016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016), 2025-07-02). `DOC-PLAIN-10`'s list is the only tell list. The six patterns of MKT-VOICE-20 hit 0 lines in 16,243 words of 18 pre-2022 landing pages.
